## Quick bio

Steven A. Sloman is Professor of Cognitive and Psychological Sciences at Brown University, where he has taught since 1992 (verified against his Researchers@Brown profile, https://vivo.brown.edu/display/ssloman, and his December 2022 CV, https://vivo.brown.edu/docs/s/ssloman_cv.pdf). He studies how people reason, make decisions, and form attitudes and beliefs; his earlier work is on categorization, inductive reasoning, dual-process theories, and causal models, and his current focus is collective cognition — "how we think as a community." He is a former Editor-in-Chief of *Cognition*, chair of the Brown faculty, and the creator of Brown's Behavioral Decision Sciences concentration. He is the author of *Causal Models: How People Think About the World and Its Alternatives* (OUP, 2005), co-author with Philip Fernbach of *The Knowledge Illusion: Why We Never Think Alone* (Riverhead, 2017) — which argues that knowledge lives in a community rather than in individual heads, and popularised the "illusion of explanatory depth" — and author of *The Cost of Conviction: How Our Deepest Values Lead Us Astray* (MIT Press, 2025), on sacred values versus consequentialist decision making.
