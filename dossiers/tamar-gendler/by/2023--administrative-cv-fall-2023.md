SECTION II: UNIVERSITY SERVICE AS FAS DEAN

A. Key accomplishments as FAS Dean, 2014-present

 Inaugural Dean of Yale’s Faculty of Arts and Sciences (FAS); developed and conceptualized role and governance
    structure, including creation and implementation of fundamental frameworks, processes and systems
 Oversaw the hiring of ~350 ladder faculty over nine years in the divisions of humanities, social science, science,
    and (until 2022) engineering and applied science for a net growth of ~60 ladder faculty (from ~660 to ~720
    ladder faculty)
 Increased the diversity and excellence of the FAS through a net growth of~40 nonwhite and ~30 URM ladder
    faculty since AY15. The youngest decile cohorts in all divisions (humanities, social sciences, sciences) are >40%
    non-white and >15% URM (by racial self-identification); the youngest decile cohorts in all divisions by gender
    self-identification are between 40/60 and 60/40; science is at 50/50 (male/female-nonbinary)
 Created, raised funds for, and recruited an inaugural FAS dean for faculty diversity and excellence
 Developed a suite of programs and policies to support of diversity, equity, inclusion and belonging for FAS staff
    (~350 clerical, technical, managerial, and professional staff members)
 Advanced university academic priorities in arts and humanities, social science, and science and engineering by
    recruiting, retaining and investing in outstanding faculty; participating in the creation of centers and institutes to
    support field-defining academic programming; and shaping major capital projects. Key FAS investments (in
    each area of university priority) include: a gift-supported renovation/construction project to house and provide
    programming for 15 humanities departments and programs at the Humanities Quadrangle (excellence in arts
    and humanities); conceptualization and creation of the gift-funded Tobin Center for Economic Policy at Yale
    and the campus-wide Data Intensive Social Science Center (data-intensive policy-relevant social science); FAS
    leadership also played central roles in securing transformative gifts and overseeing transformative investments
    in faculty and infrastructure in areas of neuroscience (WuTsai Institute), planetary solutions (Center for Natural
    Carbon Capture), data and computer science (Institute for the Foundations of Data Science), and (until 2022)
    quantum science and engineering (Yale Quantum Institute) (university science priority areas)
 Oversaw the move of more than two-thirds of FAS departments to new or fully renovated spaces and buildings on
    campus, including involvement in fundraising, planning, and execution
 Oversaw the reconceptualization, with associated renaming or restructuring, of the departments or programs in
    Statistics and Data Science (formerly Statistics), Film and Media Studies (formerly Film Studies), Theater,
    Dance, and Performance Studies (formerly Theater Studies), Ethnicity, Race, and Migration (formerly a non-
    appointing program), Italian Studies (formerly Italian language and literature), Earth and Planetary Science
    (formerly Geology and Geophysics), Jewish Studies (formerly Judaic Studies)

Created and secured funding for Scholars as Leaders, Scholars as Learners (SAL2) faculty support and leadership
   development program, providing coaching, workshops, and training for hundreds of FAS faculty
Oversaw the articulation and implementation of a new FAS promotion and tenure standard and procedure
   (FASTAP 2016); chaired more than 700 promotion and tenure cases across the divisions of humanities, social
   science, biological science, physical science, and engineering and applied science
Oversaw the development and implementation of expanded advancement and promotion systems for FAS
   instructional (non-ladder) faculty, including the introduction of new review processes, new professional
   development opportunities, and new ranks
Developed programs and incentives to support multi-disciplinary teaching and research that cuts across
   departmental, divisional and school boundaries
Worked closely with the Deans of Yale College and the Graduate School of Arts and Sciences to maintain and
   enhance excellence in teaching, learning, and mentoring; a culture of curiosity, engagement, and respect within
   and beyond the classroom; and the identification, recruitment, and support of diverse and excellent student
   bodies

B. Standing decanal responsibilities as FAS Dean, 2014-present

Oversight of FAS budget including Division of Faculty Salaries (~$200 million) and Other FAS and Academic
   Departments (~$600 million – largely pass-through)
Selection and oversight of FAS leadership team, including FAS divisional/school deans (deans of humanities,
   social science, science, and (until 2022) engineering and applied science) and other faculty deans (deans of
   diversity and faculty development, academic affairs); FAS administrative deans and central administrative staff;
   FAS department and program chairs (~50 chairs); FAS center and institute directors (~15 directors)
Selection and oversight of FAS governance committees, including: standing committees overseeing ladder faculty
   promotion and tenure, hiring and promotion of non-ladder instructional and research faculty, and governance
   of academic programs across the FAS; recurring ad hoc committees examining faculty compensation and
   faculty workload; and one-off ad hoc committees on topics ranging from the goals and ideals of undergraduate
   education in the 21st century, to the development of policies and articulation of best practices in light of the
   pandemic
Allocation and oversight of FAS departmental staff (in conjunction with FAS Director of Business Operations),
   including lead administrators, operations managers, chairs assistants, and others (~350 staff)
Oversight of interaction between FAS and central university services, including offices of strategic analysis and
   institutional research; public affairs and communications; human resources; facilities and campus development;
   public safety; sustainability; general counsel; Title IX; academic integrity; government and community relations;
   global strategy; budget and finance; university life; diversity, equity and inclusion; development and alumni
