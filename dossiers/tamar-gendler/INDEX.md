## Quick bio

Tamar Szabó Gendler (b. 1965) is the Vincent J. Scully Professor of Philosophy and Professor of Psychology and Cognitive Science at Yale, where she was the **inaugural Dean of the Faculty of Arts and Sciences from July 2014 through December 2024** (verified against her Yale FAS directory page, https://fas.yale.edu/fas-deans-office/directory/tamar-szabo-gendler, and YaleNews 2024-09-17). She earned a BA at Yale (1987) and a PhD in philosophy at Harvard (1996, advised by Robert Nozick, Derek Parfit, and Hilary Putnam), taught at Syracuse and Cornell, and returned to Yale in 2006; she chaired the Cognitive Science Program (2006–10), was the first woman to chair Yale's philosophy department (2010–13), and served as Deputy Provost for Humanities and Initiatives (2013–14). Her philosophical work joins analytic philosophy to empirical psychology: thought experiments and philosophical methodology, imagination, pretense and imaginative resistance, self-deception, the epistemic costs of implicit bias, and — most influentially — **"alief,"** the associative, automatic, arational state she introduced in two 2008 papers to explain behaviour that diverges from belief. She teaches the Open Yale Course *Philosophy and the Science of Human Nature* (PHIL 181) and its successor seminar *Public Plato: Ancient Wisdom in the Digital Age*, and speaks widely to non-academic audiences on ancient philosophy and flourishing.
