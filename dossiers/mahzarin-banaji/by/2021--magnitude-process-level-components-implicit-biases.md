# Magnitude of Process-Level Components in Implicit Biases

## Abstract (only openly available portion)

Data from the Project Implicit demonstration website, with millions of respondents across two decades, has advanced research understanding of the more spontaneous and unintentional social group attitudes in society. No method to measure mental processes is likely to be process-pure. That is, existing methods attempt to reach the constructs they are designed to test, but in actuality it is likely that they detect a variety of components reflecting both the (a) underlying mental process and (b) features of the method itself. The Implicit Association Test (IAT) is not unique in this regard. When designed to tap implicit attitudes it is known to reflect both attitudinal and non-attitudinal psychological processes (e.g., Calanchini &amp; Sherman, 2013) including (a) activation of the evaluative associations of groups (e.g., Black-bad association in the Black-White IAT), (b) inhibitory processes meant to overcome those associations, and (c) simple motor-oriented processes that turn a person’s judgments into physical responses in the task (i.e., button pressing). The question is thus raised: Which processing components underlie the magnitude of large-scale (e.g., national-level) biases? The current project answers this question using the Quadruple Process model (Quad model) in a multinomial processing tree framework applied to 14 years (although, see 'Other' for potential changes to this) of IAT data across six biases (sexuality, race, skin-tone, age, disability, body weight). For each bias, we will fit Quad model parameters to both the entire sample and subsets of demographic groups (e.g., men, women, liberals, conservatives). We will then compare the magnitude of these estimated model parameters across attitudes and demographic groups to reveal the relative contribution of each process underlying the bias. Results from those investigations will inform future preregistrations in which we will examine the changes in Quad model parameters over time, as well as their geographical associations.
