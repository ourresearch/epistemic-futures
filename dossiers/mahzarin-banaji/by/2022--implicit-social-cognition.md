# Implicit Social Cognition

## Abstract (only openly available portion)

Abstract Research on implicit social cognition has produced myriad insights into the automatic operation of social attitudes (i.e., evaluation along a good–bad dimension) and stereotypes (beliefs about the specific traits and other attributes associated with social groups). This chapter offers a summary of significant issues that are settled and those that remain open. Following a brief historical overview, addressing (1) basic findings, including the magnitude of effects and demographic variation in implicit social attitudes and stereotypes; (2) the relationship of implicit social attitudes and stereotypes with other measures, including explicit measures and intergroup behavior; (3) the neural underpinnings of implicit social attitudes and stereotypes; (4) stability and change of implicit attitudes and stereotypes, including developmental stability, situational malleability, and change over long periods of time; and (5) ongoing work on aggregate-level analyses, the representational format of implicit attitudes and stereotypes, and the role of language in shaping implicit social cognition.
