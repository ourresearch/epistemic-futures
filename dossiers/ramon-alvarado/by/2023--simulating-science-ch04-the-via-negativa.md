# The Via Negativa: Computer Simulations as Distinct

## Abstract (only openly available portion)

In this chapter I provide a series of negative arguments about what computer simulations are not. These conceptual negations serve to provide the preliminary steps towards distinguishing computer simulations from the things they have been compared to. Analyzing the assumptions and implications of some of the historical comparisons to which computer simulations have been subjected to, this chapter elucidates inconsistencies in these comparisons which show the distinct character of computer simulations. In short, this chapter shows that computer simulations are not like experiments, they are not like models and they are not like practices. Rather, as it will emerge, they are the novel technical artifacts with which each one of these other elements of inquiry can also be carried out. Unlike other analysis in the literature of computer simulations, this chapter does not rely in showing the empirical shortcomings of computer simulations to distinguish them from experimentation. Rather, it simply shows that given their functional and constitutive nature, computer simulations cannot be the same things that they are compared to, including mathematical models or broadly construed methodological practices.
