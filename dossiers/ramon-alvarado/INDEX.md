## Quick bio

Ramón Alvarado is Associate Professor of Philosophy and of the Data Science Initiative at the
University of Oregon, where he is also Data Ethics Coordinator and philosophy colloquium committee
chair, and serves on the university's AI council. He works on the philosophy and epistemology of
computational methods — computer simulations as scientific instruments, epistemic opacity in
machine learning, epistemic trust in AI, epistemic injustice in data science — and more recently on
the philosophy of loneliness. He is Editor-in-Chief of *Global Philosophy* (Springer), President
(2025) of the International Association for Computing and Philosophy, and President of the
Association for Philosophy and Computing; he co-authors the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
entry on computer simulations with Eric Winsberg, and produces the DatOS video series. He holds a
PhD in philosophy from the University of Kansas (2020), where he worked with John Symons, and MA
and BA degrees from the University of Texas at El Paso.
(Current role verified against https://cas.uoregon.edu/directory/philosophy/all/ralvarad and
https://news.uoregon.edu/expert/ramon-alvarado-department-philosophy, both retrieved 2026-08-14;
IACAP presidency against https://www.iacap.org/leadership/.)
