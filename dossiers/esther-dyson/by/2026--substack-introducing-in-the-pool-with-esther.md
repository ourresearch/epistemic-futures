# Introducing “In the pool with Esther”

## Full text

After a few random posts and a lot of re-posts, I’m finally launching my new Substack with a proper name – but not* too *much focus. “In the pool” refers to my lifelong habit of swimming, starting when I was three years old and accelerating to about 360 days a year since I was 18, with a swimming pool in the basement of my Harvard dorm. Yes, lucky me! The whole point is to get into the water and think, without the distractions of a cell phone or even a scratchpad. My wonderful editor, Christina Koukkos, has known me for long enough – I’ve told her “I’ll think about that in the pool tomorrow” many times – that she suggested the title. Indeed, my daily swim is almost as embedded in her consciousness as in mine; long ago, in a very different role, she used to call hotels for me to find out the precise hours of their pools. [...and often had to plead with the manager to open it earlier than usual – ed.]

“In the pool” nicely hints at “in the water, not on the sidelines,” and also “not necessarily in the same lane.” It invites you to jump in, too. Over time, I’ll probably talk about the labor pool and might compare things to a wading pool or cesspool (way too many of those in today’s world). But mostly, I’m inviting you to rely on me as a “[pool reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_pool)” – someone who represents a variety of journalists/news organizations in covering things not everyone has the time or access or budget to follow themselves.

My “beat” as a pool reporter reflects the Venn diagram of my interests and experiences:

-
the religion of journalism and finding out the truth (not the church of the news media)

-
a world perspective informed by frequent trips to Russia (mostly in the tech sector) from 1989 until early 2022, as well as lots of other non-touristy travel to 90-plus countries

-
25 years covering the technology industry as host of the annual PC Forum conference and editor of the *[Release 1.0](https://archive.org/details/release-1.0)* tech newsletter, which got me pool access to Bill Gates and Steve Jobs at the start, and Reid Hoffman, Mark Zuckerberg and the like at the end

-
30+ years of angel investing in startups and sitting on boards for businesses and nonprofit organizations

-
10 years coaching members of [five small US communities](https://wellville.net/) as they built their own sustainable fabric for health and well-being

-
a few detours into space travel (data centers in space, anyone? Ask your bot about “[Dyson spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyson_sphere)” for the precursor)

Most recently, I have spent much of the past year writing a book about the dynamic tension between term-limited (by mortality) humans in a world obsessed with eternal life, exponential growth and more of everything (at least for some of us), all enabled by the scale and speed of AI. What does that mean for us? Or rather, how can we *collectively* *determine* (not predict) what these dynamic tensions will produce, rather than leave things in the hands of the small group of people who determine so much for us right now? The book will be out early next year (no AI speed there!), and is called *Term Limits: Time and scale in the age of AI*, to be published by MIT Press.

### [Cui bono?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cui_bono%3F)

I like looking at the world from the perspective of an economist; that was my major at Harvard…though I spent most of my time at *[The Harvard Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/)*, the college newspaper, rather than in class. What’s the business model? What is actually motivating the behavior of people, companies, politicians…and, of course, of AI in its many instantiations (vs. incarnations of religious figures LOL)?

All this raises so many policy and cultural questions. Here are just a few I plan to cover over the next few months, mostly with specific examples. Some of them come from the book; others are prompted by what I learned writing it; and many will just be current events where I think I can provide some unique insights from – and for – the pool.

-
Couples counseling for humans and AI: It’s not *it*; it’s your *relationship* with it!!

-
Friction-free? Or value-free?

-
What’s the difference between investing and trading? (FWIW, I worked on Wall Street for five years – and reached my term limits in that business.)

-
How to make something more valuable? (Spoiler alert: Give it to someone who will love it more than you do.)

-
Redemption stories: While nothing is so good that it should last forever, neither is anything so bad that we should shun it and banish it from consideration.

-
Is GDP actually the best measure of our progress? How should we tax labor, capital and AI? (It’s complicated.)

-
What is information diabetes? (Spoiler alert: The ultimate result of consuming ultra-processed content.)

-
Theory of mind vs. theory of business models

-
Approval voting vs. ranked-choice voting (yes, politics!)

### Q&a

And finally, here’s a brief summary of the tactics of my mission, which is to provoke with big-Q questions (prompts, in AI-speak) rather than to resolve things with small-a answers:

##### Ways to learn (in order of effectiveness)

>
Read a chatbot summary

Read an original source

Engage with a brilliant teacher who asks questions

Be a brilliant teacher yourself (so you ask *yourself *questions)

Write a whole book with thoughts about those questions

*And just maybe…*

Write a Substack every other Tuesday (plus random interjections) with your own questions, and respond to those of curious readers in almost real-time

Stand by for more prompts! (And feel free to suggest your own in the comments.)
