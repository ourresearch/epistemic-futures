# An historic investment in the future of our mission, Network, and independent journalism

Today marks a historic moment for NPR and public media: NPR __has received philanthropic gifts__ totaling $113 million to invest in the future of public media in America. This extraordinary support — anchored by a landmark $80 million investment from philanthropist Connie Ballmer and an extraordinary $33 million contribution from an anonymous donor — is designed to accelerate our digital innovation and strengthen the sustainability of the NPR Network. It is a catalytic investment in building and acquiring the shared tools, technology, and services needed to better serve our audiences and support NPR Member organizations serving communities across the country.

This pivotal funding arrives at a critical moment. Last year, after more than 50 years of producing free educational, cultural, and informational programming and delivering universal service for all Americans, Congress moved to defund public media. As we approach the year anniversary of that rescission vote, its consequences are becoming increasingly evident. And while the court __recently ruled__ in favor of NPR and PBS against the Trump Administration's Executive Order — an essential victory for journalistic independence and the First Amendment — it did not reverse the devastating reality of the Congressional defunding.

But even had rescission not occurred, it would have been urgent for NPR and public media to evolve. After 50 years of delivering exceptional services to the nation, it was time to reimagine our services for the next 50 years — how we might build a sustainable, innovative, and relevant Network, native to our omnipresent digital ecosystem; a public service that sustains our common civic identity and sparks our collective curiosity.

These gifts provide the catalytic capital to drive this work. They will help NPR and the NPR Network meet the changing needs of current and future audiences, extend our mission of public service and civic support into the future, and increase our financial sustainability in the process. It is the funding necessary to build the shared national digital infrastructure and services that public media so urgently needs, and the basis for a more aligned and collaborative Network.

While these investments are extraordinary, they do not replace federal funding. The permanent loss of more than $1 billion in federal funding has created significant financial pressure across all of public media. No single benefactor can or should carry this responsibility alone. Local Member organizations — particularly those serving rural and remote parts of the country — continue to need your support.

Regular, ongoing support by local listeners, philanthropic institutions, and businesses is key to sustaining the community character of your station and hundreds of others like it across the nation, including the reporters covering city halls, the local hosts delivering the news, and emergency coverage provided during a crisis. Strong stations power the network, and differentiate public media from every other media organization.

Public media is an essential ingredient in healthy civic life. It enables strong, independent journalism that holds power to account and serves the public interest, and fosters cultural programming that celebrates our differences and connects around our commonalities. Together, these generous gifts will enable NPR to continue to deliver the nation's finest public service journalism, meeting audiences where they are today and will be in the years to come.

This is a hopeful moment for the future of the press, made possible by the unwavering dedication of supporters everywhere.

Let's get to work.

–*Katherine Maher, President and CEO, NPR*
