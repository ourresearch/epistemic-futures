## Quick bio

Adrian Dominic Sinclair Johns (b. 19 October 1965) is the Allan Grant Maclear Distinguished Service
Professor of History, the Conceptual and Historical Studies of Science, and the College at the University
of Chicago, where he has taught since 2001. Per his department page and the CHSS directory (both checked
2026-08-13: https://history.uchicago.edu/directory/adrian-johns and https://chss.uchicago.edu/directory/adrian-johns),
he is Chair of the Committee on Conceptual and Historical Studies of Science (2026-29), Interim Director of
the Morris Fishbein Center for the History of Science and Medicine, and Director of Undergraduate Studies in
HIPS; he chaired the Department of History 2022-25 and was on research leave in 2025-26. Educated at Corpus
Christi College, Cambridge (BA Natural Sciences 1987; PhD 1992), he previously taught at Cambridge, the
University of Kent, UCSD and Caltech. He works on the histories of science, the book, media and information:
*The Nature of the Book: Print and Knowledge in the Making* (1998), *Piracy: The Intellectual Property Wars
from Gutenberg to Gates* (2009), *Death of a Pirate: British Radio and the Making of the Information Age*
(2010), and *The Science of Reading: Information, Media, and Mind in Modern America* (2023), plus the
co-edited *Beyond Craft and Code: Human and Algorithmic Cultures, Past and Present* (Osiris 38, 2023, with
James Evans). His CV states he is currently writing a book on the policing of information from the Middle
Ages to the present. Awards include the 2025 Pfizer Award (History of Science Society) for *The Science of
Reading*, the 2012 Gordon J. Laing Award for *Piracy*, and for *The Nature of the Book* the Leo Gershoy,
John Ben Snow, Louis Gottschalk and SHARP prizes; he has held Guggenheim, ACLS, NEH and NSF fellowships.

### Conventions notes / ambiguities resolved in this pass
- **Pirate ebook sites were treated as out of bounds.** Complete texts of *Piracy* and *The Science of
  Reading* are on dokumen.pub. The blessed "openly reachable copy" rule names an author's site, a course
  site, or a Wayback snapshot of a formerly open page; an unauthorized upload site is not that, and it would
  be a peculiar way to build a corpus for the historian of piracy. Recorded here so the ruling is explicit.
- **His CV went to the dossier root as `cv.md`, not into `by/`.** It is his own text, but it is a reference
  apparatus rather than an argument, and it is used here the way the sidecar TSVs are.
- **Multi-speaker transcripts and roundtables** (the 9 censorship sessions, the AHR Conversation) are in
  `by/` per the roundtable rule, each with a note that much of the text is by other participants.
- **Rorotoko "cover interviews"** are author-written first-person accounts answering the site's standard
  prompts, not journalist-conducted Q&As. Kept as `type: interview` with the format explained in `notes:`.
