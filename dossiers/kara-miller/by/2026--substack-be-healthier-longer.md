# Be healthier. Longer.

*Sleep, Marriage, and GLP-1s*

## Full text

Before we get into the topic of this week’s show, a note.

I was in Bern, Switzerland a couple of weeks ago, shopping in a pretty nondescript mall, when I ran into something I had never seen before: Chinese electric cars.

As I discussed with MIT economist David Autor in our look at why China is taking American white-collar jobs (YouTube/Apple Podcasts/Spotify), China becoming a bigger player in a slew of industries: solar cells, robotics, biotech.

And in electric vehicles.

That’s true in Asia. In South America. In Europe. But not in the US.

In fact, I bet you’ve never seen a Chinese EV. (If you’ve driven in one, let me know! I’d be fascinated to hear what it was like.)

That’s because both Presidents Biden and Trump have aggressively protected US car manufacturers.

As Autor - who has seen a Chinese EV - said of the Xiomi SU7:

I have to say it was it was an amazing car. It looked kind of like a much handsomer Tesla Model Y, that had much better electronics and much better interior appointments. It was a handsome car. And it is $20,000 cheaper than the US competitor.

My German is pretty bad (so feel free to correct me) but that squares with what I saw.

(Pause for a moment - if you would - to imagine calm-and-composed Swiss shoppers wondering why a crazy American lady is excitedly snapping photos of Chinese EVs.)

EVs could be half of the worldwide market in less than a decade. And, as The New York Times recently reported, the fact that our government has insulated our car companies may, ironically, lead to their decline.

OK, onwards from Switzerland:

This week’s show is with Princeton molecular biologist Coleen Murphy, who spends her time researching how we can live longer, healthier lives. She’s the author of How We Age (Princeton University Press).

[You can see the show here on YouTube, and listen to it on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

A few weeks ago, I talked with author Saul Justin Newman about why there may be fewer 100-year-olds in the world than we think. (Think: pension fraud, bad record keeping.)

But Murphy focuses on what scientists know about extending life - and she notes that there have been lots of exciting developments in the last few years.

What actions can we take without pharmaceuticals? What drugs are changing longevity? And what’s in the pharmaceutical pipeline?

One of the topics Murphy talks about is the importance of getting enough sleep. Recent work shows that getting enough sleep (and high-quality sleep) appears to contribute significantly to longevity.

[You can see the show here on YouTube, and listen to it on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

Murphy and I also explore what research reveals about calorie restriction, intermittent fasting, marriage, GLP-1s, exercise, and more. And she talks about her work on combatting cognitive decline (as separate from Alzheimer’s).
