# Just "riding it down"

*Why white collar jobs are heading to China, with MIT's David Autor*

## Full text

In the late 1800s, a Bavarian Jewish immigrant (at the time, there was no Germany, only a “German Confederation”) named Levi Strauss started making tough, practical blue jeans in the Bay Area.

The jeans were a dynamite combination of American ingenuity and manufacturing know-how, and Strauss would become enormously wealthy. (Fun fact: One of his heirs - Dan Goldman - currently represents New York in Congress.)

More than 125 years later - in 2004 - the company that bears Strauss’ name shuttered the last of its US manufacturing plants, opting to shift production overseas.

Which didn’t come as a surprise. When Levi’s closed their plants in San Antonio, it was part of an enormous migration of blue collar jobs out of the US.

Often, those jobs landed in China, where salaries were much lower and the labor pool was much bigger. The transition came to be known as The China Shock, and it would have profound economic and political consequences (see: The Tea Party, the rise of President Trump).

Now, says MIT economist David Autor - who became famous, in part, for explaining that first China Shock - we’ve mostly ignored the next China Shock, which will impact white collar jobs.

[You can see the show here on YouTube, and listen to on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

In this week’s show, I talk with Autor about whose jobs are at stake and why we haven’t paid attention to this threat.

One of Autor’s deep concerns is that our “political system has the attention span of a squirrel on cocaine.”

So what could happen if we don’t get our acts together?

“I think it’s the path of just riding it down,” he told me. “Not taking constructive action. And just watching sector by sector [as we] lose leadership and lose innovative capacity.”

Autor understands how technology changes the world, alters the job landscape, and upends economies. I always feel smarter when I get to listen to his take on what’s ahead.
