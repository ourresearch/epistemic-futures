# College's value, elite waitlists, and marriage markets

*Does college really make you successful?*

## Full text

Hi Friends,

Back in 2017, a team of economists found that “children whose parents are in the top 1% of the income distribution are 77 times more likely to attend an Ivy League college than those whose parents are in the bottom income quintile.”

This was a stunning finding.

The economists - led by Raj Chetty, who is now at Harvard - weren’t talking about the success of the richest 10 or 15% of Americans. They were pinpointing just the top 1%, who were - clearly - accessing elite institutions at shockingly high rates.

But this finding, in turn, raises a fascinating question:

Is it Princeton (for example) that makes you successful? Or does Princeton do a good job of accepting people who are likely to be successful?

(Of course, there might be multiple paths to “success.” Success could come from hard work and innate intelligence. Or it could result from the fact that students’ families are well-connected and have a lot of cash to fall back on. Or it could be some combination of the two.)

And there are similar, broader questions you could ask about college.

[You can see the show here on YouTube, and listen to on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

“We’re bombarded every day by these statistics in the newspaper and in college guides and the popular press that tell us, ‘Hey, people who go to college earn a lot more money than people who don’t go to college,’” says Jack Mountjoy, an Associate Professor of Economics at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business.

And that’s true, he says. But “the next sentence or the next paragraph in that article is going to be: ‘Therefore, people should go to college because college must cause you to have higher earnings.’ And those two things are not necessarily logically connected.”

Why?

“The types of individuals who go to college are, on average, quite different from the types of individuals who don’t go to college.”

So Mountjoy - who’s the guest on the show this week - tried to get to the bottom of the question: Is college worth it? And the goal - hopefully - is not to mix in extraneous factors like wealth and connectedness.

On the show, we also talk about what happens to you if you get off the waitlist of an elite college. Does it change your life? Or not really?

And we talk about college as a marriage market.

I had never considered the finding-your-partner angle, but Mountjoy argues: we should. What if college affects who you marry? (Often, it does.) In fact, what if your major affects who you marry? (Often, it does.)
