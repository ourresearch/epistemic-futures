# Everyone knows it's a problem. Now what?

*The research on how to fix traffic - and why most politicians ignore it.*

## Full text

Dear Friends,

This week’s show is about a problem so ubiquitous that most people have given up on the idea of solving it: traffic.

In a study of commuting more than 30 years ago - and in many studies since - researchers pointed out adverse impacts on “blood pressure, tolerance for frustration, negative mood, and overall life satisfaction.” Even “low stress” people, they found, could get really ticked off by bad traffic.

We’ve known that traffic is terrible - and terrible for you - for a long time.

Not only is it stress-inducing, stop-and-go traffic produces tremendous amounts of pollution. And that, as this week’s guest Michael Manville notes, poisons those who live near major highways.

Manville - who’s the Chair of Urban Planning at UCLA’s Luskin School of Public Affairs - points out that these tend to be poorer people, and the pollutants contribute to an array of conditions, including low birth weight, a major cause of infant mortality in the US.

And then there’s the fact that, generally speaking, time is money. When you lose time to traffic, there’s a lot at stake. If you pay a babysitter, getting home earlier could save you money. For many people - whether you work at a pharmacy or in a law firm - another hour on the job is more money in your pocket. And even for salaried employees, the ability to get more done at work, rather than squandering your time on the highway, could prove hugely beneficial over time.

Thing is: we’ve had the answer for how to fix bad traffic at least since the 1970s, when Singapore figured out how to keep their highways flowing at about 50 miles per hour almost all the time.

Scholars have tried to convince politicians that it was worth following Singapore’s lead. But both Republicans and Democrats have (mostly) been uninterested.

So far, the motley group of politicians who have taken the plunge range from libertarians to socialists. But when they do decide to fix traffic, it turns out to be popular.

So what do we do? And why do scholars think it’s the only way to fix traffic?
