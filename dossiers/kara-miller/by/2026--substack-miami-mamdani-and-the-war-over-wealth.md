# Miami, Mamdani, and the War over Wealth

*Why progressivism is on the rise*

## Full text

Many of the superwealthy pay almost no taxes.

Which has ticked off a raft of states: California. Massachusetts. New York. (Just to name a few.) If you make - or just have - a lot of money, those states want their share.

To which plenty of billionaires have four words: I’m moving to Miami.

Will this empty the coffers of blue states? Will it boost metros from Nashville to Dallas to Orlando?

Richard Florida, a scholar of cities who has advised legions of them, joins me to talk about who’s on the rise, who’s in trouble, and why he thinks New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani is the harbinger of a new progressive movement.

[You can see the show here on YouTube, and listen to it on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

Mamdani, Florida argues, has done something that Democrats need to do more broadly: he has united service workers and “the creative class,” which includes everyone from lawyers to web designers.

Both of these groups feel like life is increasingly unaffordable, and they resent billionaires for living it up, while - frequently - skirting taxes.

After the Trumpian embrace of billionaires - heck, his own multi-billion dollar net worth is being enhanced all the time - Florida believes we will soon see the pendulum swing back. And Mamdani is a sign that the shift is already underway.

Plus, we talk about what cities are most likely to suffer from AI, whether California should worry that a million people have left in recent years, and why legions of corporate headquarters have moved to Dallas.

Boston College’s Ray Madoff explained to me (YouTube/Apple) why upper-middle class people in blue states often pay 50% of their income in taxes (and we’re talking federal, state, and payroll taxes combined). Think doctors and lawyers. But billionaires frequently pay less than 10% of their income in taxes. It will make you mad.

If you get elected president, your salary will be $400,000, which doesn’t go as far as it used to. Fortunately, if you have some side hustles, you can rake in another $2.2 billion.

Richard Florida says that plug-and-play cities - like Miami and Dubai - have some serious vulnerabilities. One of them is that when the going gets tough… well, you know. He writes in the NYT about what happened in Dubai when the bombs started falling.
