# The research on socializing will change you

*The stranger connection*

## Full text

Are you above average?

Probably.

About 65% of Americans think they are more intelligent than the average person.

And up to 80% of us think we are better-than-average drivers.

In fact there’s lots of domains where the impossible is true: most of us are better than most of us.

But there is one skill where we tend to be “relatively pessimistic,” according to research: our ability to have a conversation. There, according to a team including Cornell’s Thomas Gilovich, “many people report that engaging in informal conversation with anyone other than close friends and family makes them anxious”

What’s striking about this is the mountain of data showing that striking up conversations almost always makes you happier.

[You can see the show here on YouTube, and listen to on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

This week on the show, I talk with University of Chicago behavioral scientist Nicholas Epley, who says he has been completely transformed by this research. He now strikes up conversations on planes, subways, trains… wherever.

And we talk about both the health and happiness bumps you get when you are A Little More Social - which is the title of his new book. (Did you know that being lonely is on par with smoking 15 cigarettes a day, when it comes to increasing the risk of an earlier death?)

But our pessimism that conversations will go awry is considerable. And it discourages us from starting them.

Epley talks about how you get over this, whether introverts and extroverts should think differently about the problem, and its impact on political divisions.

[You can see the show here on YouTube, and listen to on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

One tip, if you’re looking to strike up conversations, Epley says, is not to assume that there are some magic words to say.

Rather, recognize that everyone is inherently interesting. They all know - as Bill Nye has said - something that you don’t know. And approaching people with curiosity can be hugely helpful to getting the conversation going.

If you want to read more of Epley’s academic papers: https://www.nicholasepley.com/publications

Don’t hesitate to tell me how those conversations go, as you attempt to start them!!
