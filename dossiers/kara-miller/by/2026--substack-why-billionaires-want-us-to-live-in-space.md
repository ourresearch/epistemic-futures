# Why billionaires want us to live in space

*An astrophysicist has questions*

## Full text

Hi Friends,

In November of 2024, just a few days after his re-election, President Trump chose Elon Musk and Vivek Ramaswamy to run DOGE - the Department of Government Efficiency.

DOGE slashed thousands of government jobs and, in many ways, reordered the federal bureaucracy (without the help of Ramaswamy, who left DOGE almost immediately after Trump was inaugurated in January of 2025).

So what drove the decisions of Elon Musk, DOGE’s leader? How does Musk think about long-term planning, and how civilization, more generally, should move forward?

This week on It Turns Out, I talk with astrophysicist Adam Becker, the author of More Everything Forever: AI Overlords, Space Empires, and Silicon Valley’s Crusade to Control the Fate of Humanity.

[Watch the show on YouTube, or listen on Apple Podcasts and Spotify]

In it, he argues that Musk - and a smattering of other billionaires - are wrongly focused on the notion that our destiny is in the stars.

As Musk’s SpaceX website proudly proclaims: “SpaceX was founded under the belief that a future where humanity is out exploring the stars is fundamentally more exciting than one where we are not.”

Here’s the catch, says Becker: It’s exceedingly unlikely that the future of humanity is in space. Believing that there’s a viable way to put “a trillion humans” - Jeff Bezos’ idea - in space colonies simply demonstrates a lack of knowledge about the harsh constraints of space.

And, Becker argues, it offers a way of ignoring some of the most pressing issues here on Earth - from global warming to, yes, a functioning government seeking to remedy our most serious problems.

One of the problems - as Becker sees it - is that we’ve glorified computer science, at the expense of other disciplines. Which can lead people to believe the world is much simpler than it really is.

Do you agree with Becker’s take? Let me know.
