### 2026-07-09 — https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7481107803382931456/

AI gateways meter the model side of an agent so they can charge you for tokens, but nothing leaves a lasting trace of the content and other inputs an agent used to do its work.

Ex-GitHub CEO Thomas Dohmke argued that session logs are now the second most important artefact in software development (link to the product launch article in comments).

I think he is right - though I would generalise it into something we all already know: showing your work and citing your sources have always been the second most important thing in knowledge work.

When AI generates anything it should record its sources. That includes all of the content, code, and skills it has ingested along the way. It isn't just a matter of attribution - how else do we know what content was valuable? What skill was useful, and which one led the agent down a token-burning doom loop?

Which leaves us with a need to measure the usage and value of content, data, and skills as they influence and guide an agent.

Jonathan Roberts keeps telling me I use lots of words to make the same simple point: better inputs make better AI. AI systems shouldn't be the exception to the rules about citing those inputs.

Of the many great conversations at Foo Camp last weekend, talking to Ilan S., Tim O'Reilly, Raul Castro Fernandez, Lucky Gunasekara and others about skills as the "structured extraction of organisational tribal knowledge" has got me thinking more deeply about what we can do with standardised reporting about how AI systems work.
