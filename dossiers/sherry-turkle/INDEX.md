## Quick bio

Sherry Turkle (b. 1948) is the Abby Rockefeller Mauzé Professor of the Social Studies of Science
and Technology at MIT and founding director (2001) of the MIT Initiative on Technology and Self;
she has held the Mauzé chair since 1999 and has been on the MIT faculty since 1976 (verified
against her CV dated July 2026, https://sherryturkle.mit.edu/files/2026/07/Turkle__CV__July_2026.pdf,
and her site https://sherryturkle.mit.edu/). She holds a joint Harvard PhD in sociology and
personality psychology (1976) and is a licensed clinical psychologist. Across a 45-year career she
has written a sequence of books on the psychology of computation and computer culture —
*Psychoanalytic Politics* (1978; 2nd ed. MIT Press 2024), *The Second Self* (1984; 20th-anniversary
ed. 2005), *Life on the Screen* (1995), *Alone Together* (2011), *Reclaiming Conversation* (2015;
10th-anniversary ed. 2025), and the memoir *The Empathy Diaries* (2021) — plus the MIT Press
"object trilogy" she edited (*Evocative Objects* 2007, *Falling for Science* 2008, *The Inner
History of Devices* 2008) and *Simulation and Its Discontents* (2009). Her current work is on what
she calls **"artificial intimacy"** — chatbots and social robots presented as friends, therapists
and lovers — the subject of her forthcoming book *Artificial Intimacy: Who We Become When We Talk
to Machines* (Little, Brown, announced for 29 September 2026).
