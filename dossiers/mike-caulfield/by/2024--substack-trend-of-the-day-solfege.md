# Trend of the Day: Solfege

*Another post about search trends and NYT Games*

## Full text

If you were doing the Connections puzzle last night, you may have wondered, like me, what the heck Solfege meant.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!0Wq0!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F38e23c7e-83c2-4cd5-b55a-ee8a14ebca2d_1030x654.png>)

You weren’t alone! In their quest to figure out what this word was, either during or after the puzzle, many people turned to Google: 

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Z6NU!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fc46132a0-3002-4747-a7e0-1c1ecc58529e_1733x508.png>)Search trend for Solfege, showing it rocketing up last night when the puzzle was released.

To give you a relative scale on that, about as many people searched solfege at its peak as searched for _celine_ during her song at the Olympics (though the persistent interest means there were more searches in the past seven days for _celine_ overall.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!fWQK!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd606974a-c77e-4a43-b915-841a86c423bb_1768x539.png>)Search volume of celine vs. solfege, which peak at different times but peak out at close to the same level.

The related queries hold some interesting data. Unsurprisingly, many of the people who had questions about solfege also asked for hints, or had questions about _libra_ as well:

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!GKoN!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F74553ed3-3846-48d8-ade5-bef6c6d7cfae_1759x833.png>)Top 5 related queries for solfege — solfege libra, libra, libra meaning, definition libra, connections hint.

Also the fact some also searched fish and alley (not particularly obscure words) indicates to me that some people when they play connections maybe looking up words they know to generate ideas on how they might be grouped. This might also be behind the weird top result of solfege libra as well — not really sure there. 

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!-zfI!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F4b8f2cc1-219a-49d1-8c81-298e7b3be17a_1778x826.png>)Related queries 16-20 — fish, solfege fish, alley, wordle hint, coliseum meaning.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!YrZc!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F029b55e4-bc72-41d2-916b-f76b9f6d25fd_1773x866.png>)Related queries 11-15, including _solfege libra justice_ and _solfege justice_ as well as _teapot_ and _coliseum_

Takeways? Again, nothing big, but stuff I find interesting at least:

  * Assisting in the solution of word games is a significant use of Google

  * People that ask for hints in one NYT game are somewhat likely to ask for hints in another

  * In addition to hints, people use Google during word games to get definitions, and also generate ideas — including in this case by putting words together to see (I think?) if they share any properties.

I’ll probably dig into this again at some point, I find the _solfege libra justice_ search fascinating…
