The fact-check at the link is more extensive, but the summary SIFT Toolbox gives is here:

> A 2014 study published in Cell Stem Cell by researchers at the University of Southern California found that cycles of prolonged fasting (48-72 hours) in mice triggered significant changes in hematopoietic stem cells, which are responsible for generating blood and immune cells ([Cell Stem Cell](<https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1934590914001519>)). The fasting cycles caused a decrease in circulating IGF-1 levels and PKA activity, which prompted stem cells to switch from a dormant state to a state of self-renewal ([USC Today](<https://today.usc.edu/fasting-triggers-stem-cell-regeneration-of-damaged-old-immune-system/>)). When mice were re-fed after fasting, researchers observed regeneration of certain white blood cells.
> 
> The study included a small pilot clinical trial in humans undergoing chemotherapy, which showed potential protective effects against chemotherapy-induced toxicity. However, the researchers emphasized that more clinical studies would be needed, and any such dietary intervention should only be undertaken under medical supervision ([Science Daily](<https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/06/140605141507.htm>)). The research did not demonstrate "regeneration of the entire immune system" as claimed in viral social media posts, but rather showed specific mechanisms affecting certain immune cell populations.
