# Far more people are searching for "Trump rally" to prove Google is biased than are searching for information using "Trump rally"

*It's kind of funny actually?*

## Full text

At 11:02 Pacific Time today, Elon Musk demonstrated he doesn’t understand news search:

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Kw-l!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd5dab966-1dfc-40da-85d0-25565c78dd13_1109x915.png>)Musk screenshots a search for _Trump rally_ without quotes that returns a news topic about Harris’s rally.

The reason for this result has to do with how news stories are integrated into the SERP. I don’t want to get into that here, but suffice it to say that that news panel only pops out when a significant set of recent stories match your search terms (or synonyms for them). It’s trying to figure out whether the terms are associated with a recent event or set of breaking news stories and then show you stories about those grouped by topic — not show you a collection of the best matches for the _terms_. Needless to say if there are a bunch of recent news stories about today's rally where Harris talks about Trump you are going to see those stories suggested if you search _Trump rally,_ and not, say, stories about last week's Trump rally, or stories about a rally that hasn't happened yet.1

Anyway, people lost their minds a bit on Twitter, because this plays into a long-standing narrative that Google is suppressing favorable results for Republicans, dating back to the furor over some (in my opinion) [poorly designed research](<https://www.politico.com/magazine/story/2015/08/how-google-could-rig-the-2016-election-121548/>) back in 2015 about the supposed “Search Engine Effect”. Again, I’ll leave explaining that to others. But you had a flood of people executing this search (_Trump rally_) themselves and then posting their screenshots “verifying” the phenomenon as in this shared example from around 2 p.m.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!WbDF!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F78294f14-98ee-47bc-96e5-a7b7b70d716f_918x675.jpeg>)Another screenshot showing _Trump rally_ results that feature Harris rally stories.

To a much, much lesser degree you found people also searching to “disprove” Musk, as with this constructed but typical example where a user searches the same terms after Trump’s evening rally, and gets different more Trump-focused results, because, well, there are more news stories about a rally after it happens than before.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!ejJ1!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F92c9dbd4-f647-4fde-8ffc-0790ed4888dd_752x704.png>)_Trump rally_ search with Trump focused results shared with text saying this shows Musk was faking it.

What I wanted to note was this — here’s what the search trend for Trump rally looked like today. That first spike there is from when Musk posted his Tweet.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!b0MW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F7c2afb05-00c7-4916-9fb4-4d24fbf4a642_1708x588.png>)Search trend for Trump rally showing almost no searches then a radical jump up at around the time of the Musk tweet.

And it’s a big spike! In that first period, nearly one out of three searches containing _Trump_ contained _rally_. That ratio was repeated during a later spike at 3:16 p.m as well.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!vx67!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe3b41067-5cd4-44b9-b817-6912508ccc23_1687x694.png>)Trend for _Trump_ compared to _Trump rally_

But here’s the funny thing. _Trump rally_ — with the exception of in the period immediately after the assassination attempt mid-July — is not usually that common a search. Here’s the last seven days:

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HBB-!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F335a3a23-92d2-475f-a83d-674fe80a71be_1707x694.png>)_Trump rally_ over past seven days 

When Trump does have a rally, there are little spikes, as there was on the 27th above for his Minnesota rally. If you look at the related queries for these it’s not clear how many people searching are looking for news. A lot of what people seem to be searching for is where to watch it online, and what time it starts. 

In fact, by the time the rally starts, the peak on the 27th is well into its decline, a good indication that many people were not looking for news coverage (an _informational_ search) but to find a stream of it online or on TV (a _transactional_ search). The searches for news coverage of what happened using those terms are later and of lesser volume. (Again, the exception to this rule is when there is a major news event tied to the rally, such as after the assassination attempt).

But we are in a situation right now — today at least — where far more people are typing in these terms to “prove” that Google is biased (replicating the recipe Musk gave them) than were actually searching _Trump rally_ to begin with.

For what it’s worth, Trump supporters aren’t the only people to fall into this trap. I’ve seen misinformation researchers run Mechanical Turk tests where the majority of people searching on a “problematic” query were people _in the test_ , due to the query being so artificial and rare. But that’s for another day.
