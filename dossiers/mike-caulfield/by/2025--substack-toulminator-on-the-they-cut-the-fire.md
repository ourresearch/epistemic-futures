# Toulminator on the "They cut the Fire Department to Fund the Police" Claim

## Full text

Since I’m sure at some point people will ask if the [Toulminator](<https://chatgpt.com/g/g-677e08bde7588191a699e8e89e26b123-toulminator>) (my little ChatGPT argument analysis experiment) is politically biased, I decided to try it on a piece of liberal misinformation about the Los Angeles fires, namely that the Fire Department budget was cut to fund the police (and presumably that’s why the fires are out of control). 

In this case the answer it gives benefits from having checked a couple things before it in the same chat session (which is how it knows people have talked about a $50 million increase), but the broader check is impressive without that number, and it shows it will go through its paces on whatever you give it.

As always, you should not trust raw LLM output — take the authority of this as something at something like the level you would take from, I don’t know — maybe the in the moment reaction of a smart generalist riffing on a topic where they don’t really know the details.

**Response:**

_This post makes two distinct claims: that the Los Angeles fire department's budget was cut by $23 million to increase the LAPD budget by $138 million, and that prison labor was used for firefighting. Here's an analysis of the argument._

* * *

### _**Claim**_

  1.  _LA cut $23 million from the fire department's budget._

  2. _The cut was made to fund a $138 million increase for the LAPD._

  3. _Prison labor was utilized for firefighting during this time._

* * *

### _**Grounds (Evidence)**_

  1. _**Fire Department Budget Cut:** The video text explicitly claims a $23 million budget reduction._

  2. _**LAPD Budget Increase:** A claim of a $138 million increase in LAPD funding is presented without specific evidence in the video._

  3. _**Prison Labor for Firefighting:** It is mentioned that prisoners were used for firefighting, implying resource exploitation or mismanagement._

_**Backing for Evidence:**_

  * _For the fire department budget cut: This would require official LA budget records for verification (documentation)._

  * _For the LAPD increase: Similarly, city budget documentation or announcements would substantiate this claim._

  * _For prison labor: California does have a known history of using incarcerated individuals in firefighting, so this could fall under common knowledge or public records._

_**Questions on Evidence Credibility:**_

  * _Were these budget changes directly connected, or are they coincidental (e.g., LAPD increase unrelated to fire department funding)?_

  * _Is the $23 million claim accurate and reflective of the total fire department budget?_

  * _Was prison labor employed specifically in Los Angeles, or is this a statewide practice conflated with local claims?_

* * *

### _**Warrant**_

 _The argument assumes that a cut to the fire department budget directly funds the increase in LAPD funding, and that using prison labor is a deliberate strategy to reduce fire department costs._

_**Backing for Warrant:**_

  * _**Budget Reallocation Logic:** This is implied but unsubstantiated. Budgets are not always fungible or linked linearly._

  * _**Criticism of Prison Labor Practices:** This is generally supported by public discourse about the ethics of using incarcerated individuals in hazardous roles, often for minimal compensation._

* * *

### _**Rebuttals**_

  1.  _**No Direct Link Between Budgets:** Without explicit documentation tying the fire department cut to the LAPD increase, this claim is speculative._

  2. _**Overall Fire Department Funding Increase:** As per earlier claims, the fire department’s overall budget increased by $50 million, potentially contradicting this $23 million cut claim._

  3. _**Prison Labor Context:** While prison labor in firefighting is well-documented in California, it may not specifically apply to LA’s response or budgeting decisions._

  4. _**Timeline Issues:** The claims about budget changes and their effects need to align temporally. Were these changes in the same fiscal year?_

* * *

### _**External Evidence**_

  1.  _**Official Budget Records:** Examination of LA’s budget for the relevant fiscal year would clarify changes to the fire department and LAPD funding._

  2. _**Reports on Prison Labor Use:** Confirming whether incarcerated individuals were specifically deployed in LA firefighting efforts would provide necessary context._

  3. _**Historical Budget Trends:** A broader view of fire and police budget allocations in LA could contextualize these claims._

* * *

### _**Summary**_

 _**Verdict:** **Lacks evidence and partially misleading.**_

_The claim of a $23 million budget cut to the fire department appears contradicted by official accounts of a $50 million year-over-year increase. The allegation of funding reallocation to the LAPD remains unsubstantiated without clear documentation. The use of prison labor is a plausible but generalized claim, requiring specificity for LA. Overall, this post combines potentially valid concerns about resource allocation with speculative or exaggerated framing._
