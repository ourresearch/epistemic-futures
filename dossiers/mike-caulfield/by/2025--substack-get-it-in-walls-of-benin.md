# Get It In: Walls of Benin

*How pushing the claim or artifact to the LLM for analysis can bring insights you weren't expecting*

## Full text

One of my suggestions for verification around the [“get it in” move](<https://mikecaulfield.substack.com/p/get-it-in-track-it-down-follow-up>) is to just push the claim or artifact you are looking at up to the AI. Don’t think of a query, don’t put in search terms, don’t start by typing a well-constructed prompt. 

There’s a couple reasons for that. The biggest, of course, is if you fret about what to ask the model even for a second, the likelihood you don’t check something you think you _should_ check goes up. A little friction in a process has a big impact. 

Another reason, however, is often by not asking at specific question at first you given the model more room to zoom in on unexpected things. In this case an artifact asserting some textual claims about the length of the Walls of Benin turns out to have an issue I wasn’t even thinking about. 

Since I don’t want to “burn” some of my teaching examples, I’ll not be describing these walkthroughs in the text of these posts. Apologies about that, but I’ve learned the hard way that if I type up the “findings” on one of these examples on this blog on a Thursday quite often the results in AI Mode or searches will have this blog as a source by the following Tuesday, which kind of defeats the purpose of developing workshop examples. Hopefully keeping the walkthroughs in the videos will allow me to show them here without destroying the utility of my prompt library. We’ll see, I guess. 

Check out the video though. Five minutes long and a kind of cool and unexpected thing happens at minute two. And stay tuned for more examples you can use in your teaching.
