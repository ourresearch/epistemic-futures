# Why teens are wired for risk

## Full text

[Why teens are wired for risk](<http://www.cnn.com/2011/10/19/health/mental-health/teen-brain-impulses/index.html?hpt=hp_c2>)

Kind of important for higher education to think about, no? 

> Scientists typically refer to “the teenage brain” in 13- to 17-year-olds, but that doesn’t mean that college students are totally “adults” yet. In fact, research from the National Institutes of Health has shown, the prefrontal cortex, a region of the brain associated with inhibition of risky behavior, _doesn ’t get fully developed until age 25_. The connections between the prefrontal cortex and other areas of the brain are also developing in teenagers. And a number of deep structures in the brain are influenced by changes in hormones, which may lead to heightened emotions.
