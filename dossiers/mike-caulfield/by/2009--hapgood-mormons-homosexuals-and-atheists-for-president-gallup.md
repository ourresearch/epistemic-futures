# Mormons, Homosexuals, and Atheists for President (Gallup)

## Full text

Just some data re: my post the other day, from [Gallup, February 2007](<http://www.gallup.com/poll/26611/Some-Americans-Reluctant-Vote-Mormon-72YearOld-Presidential-Candidates.aspx>). Keep in mind the question here is “Would you vote for the person in your political party if they were of belief/orientation/gender/age X?” The difference from 100% represents the number of people that would either not vote at all or vote against their party if their party ran a candidate of this sort.

_Between now and the 2008 political conventions, there will be discussion about the qualifications of presidential candidates — their education, age, religion, race, and so on. If your party nominated a generally well-qualified person for president who happened to be …, would you vote for that person?_

|  **Yes, would  
vote for** |  **No, would not  
vote for**  
---|---|---  
|  % |  %  
Catholic |  95 |  4  
Black |  94 |  5  
Jewish |  92 |  7  
A woman |  88 |  11  
Hispanic |  87 |  12  
Mormon |  72 |  24  
Married for the third time |  67 |  30  
72 years of age |  57 |  42  
A homosexual |  55 |  43  
An atheist |  45 |  53  
  
.

I’m not really arguing anything here, this is just interesting to me. I’m always curious what theists think atheists would do in office that would be so different. “Well, seeing as there is no God, I think I will use my newfound power to start a White House harem! And after that I’ll force people to worship ME! Bwhahaha!”

People amuse me. There really is this insane belief out there that if people all of a sudden didn’t believe in God there would be riots in the street as everyone tried to steal TV sets and break into banks and have public sex. Because all that apparently stops people from being good is belief in God — so society would fall apart.
