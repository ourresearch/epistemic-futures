# Trend of the Day: US medals count

*A short note on a popular search and a useful result*

## Full text

Searching for “medal count” in Google gets you an official, up-to-date count. It’s pretty cool.

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!j6-6!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F33bc2c8f-593e-43e8-a889-0ac2ade245f6_877x769.png>)The medal count tab on the Google Knowledge Graph SERP element

Do people end up using that a lot? Yes, they do! Here’s level of that search query against one of the biggest searches of the early Olympics — searches on Celine Dion.

Medal count has maintained a stable level greater than the very peak of Celine searches, and its recent peak is double the Celine volume.

Or, if this helps you, currently the daily searches for medal count exceed searches for Kamala Harris:

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!bR0a!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F93ddb611-2ae2-4b3d-9b78-e515d5ff4691_1701x683.png>)Medal count against Harris terms on Google Trends. The current level of searches for medal count exceeds Harris searches.

People in the U.S. start with that query early, and it seems to have accelerated recently as the U.S. and China have been neck and neck.

It’s a good example of how searches for things like sports scores or team rankings dominate search more than many realize. (It’s also an example of a search that provides a nice little non-LLM result that — at least in my opinion — is 100 times more useful than a lot of the LLM stuff.) 

There are many little specialized panels like this in Google, in addition to the thousands of lists (like the one [described at the end of this article](<https://mikecaulfield.substack.com/p/still-alive>)) generated off of the Knowledge Graph. They form a big part of people’s search experience, and receive relatively little mention in the literature or reporting. I document them here because I want to broaden people’s idea of what search is and how people interact with it.
