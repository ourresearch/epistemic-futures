# Covered Holocaust Monument

*Was a covered Holocaust monument a sign of rising antisemitism?*

## Full text

[](<https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!2Gtz!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe4c87e4-40cb-4fd0-ae8c-76a6825e434f_950x1219.jpeg>)

Monuments (and the things done to them) often figure into arguments. At core, the existence of a monument to something implies a sort of value for that thing. This cuts both ways. Many saw the continued existence of Confederate monuments in the U.S. as evidence of tacit support for a politics of white supremacy. When those monuments were pulled down, others presented this as evidence of an erasure of heritage. (Those arguments are not equivalent, but that was the structure of engagement around the issue).

We can probably do this better, but I just wanted to get something down before jumping in and learning what this is all about. Note that the article has at the top “After an officer warns campaigner for being openly Jewish”. So part of this is an _[incremental argument](<https://mikecaulfield.substack.com/p/the-structure-of-a-diedsuddenly-argument>)_ as discussed in a previous post. This act is seen as the latest of a series of events that taken together show that rising antisemitism is all around us.

Now that we’ve got that part sketched out, let’s find out what is going on here. Keep in mind that what makes this compelling is its evidence of an antisemitic trend among authorities, who ware portrayed as caving to the “woke mob”.

So we jump into the article that provides backing, and the first thing we notice is that the article reads a bit differently. The memorial is not being hidden out of shame, but out of fear that a large pro-Palestine event that is happening near it might result in vandalism. This is a slightly different argument — they are not covering up out of shame, but out of protection. 

This could still be seen as slightly weaker evidence of of antisemitism — after all, if this fear is new and unprecedented, if we suddenly have to worry about the defacement of the monument then that’s not great.

So what’s the core of a “rising tide” argument? It’s that things are increasing in intensity or quantity. And it’s paired with this other thing here, where the authorities are seen as caving. This is the “shame” piece of it. The cops here that are supposedly backing down, and that is (I think) an indication of the intensity of the situation as presented, and an approach that is perhaps too afraid of offending the protestors. So this isn’t perfect, but it’s my redrawing of the argument:

This feels like a cleaner version to me. The monument here is dual evidence of both the rising intensity of the attacks and the state’s unwillingness to take some bolder form of action. The idea is something along the lines of “Why does the monument have to be the one to hide?” 

When we look at it through this lens, we do find a number of things that undermine the strength of the evidence. 

  * According to the park service, the monument has been protected this way before, so it’s not as novel as presented. This is only introduced in the Daily Mail article after over two dozen paragraphs that treat this as unprecedented. Additionally, it is the park service, not the police that cover it.

  * Also notably, the cops are not backing down here — it looks like during the upcoming pro-Palestine March they will be standing guard. 

Both these elements seem to me to undermine the strength of this as evidence that we are at a unique inflection point., or that the authorities are backing down from confronting antisemitism. 

In the end, though, these things do not seem to dismantle the argument as much as shift it. This example loses much force by not being unprecedented, and the headline is clearly rage-bait. The police angle here seems wholly false. This does not suggest, as the headline does, that the “police are cowed”.

But I can also see where having the additional context pointed out might make someone wonder whether they should feel comforted that this might be “simply” evidence of _prevalent_ or _persistent_ antisemitism, whether it is rising or not. Is the presentation here misleading? I think it is, in that it creates a sense of novelty and momentum and police attitude that the evidence may not support. But even those who come to the conclusion that this event is more mundane than novel may find that cold comfort.
