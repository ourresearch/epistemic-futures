# Increased Structure and Active Learning Reduce the Achievement Gap in Introductory Biology

## Full text

[Increased Structure and Active Learning Reduce the Achievement Gap in Introductory Biology](<http://www.sciencemag.org/content/332/6034/1213.abstract>)

It’s yet another article in _Science_ dealing with pedagogy, and if the note in [this news brief](<http://www.sciencenews.org/view/generic/id/330803/title/News_in_Brief_Humans_>) under “Teaching to Achieve” is right, it looks like the second vindication of Peer Instruction in _Science_ in the the space of two months. According to the [brief](<http://www.sciencenews.org/view/generic/id/330803/title/News_in_Brief_Humans_>): “A highly structured course design that included peer instruction and peer-graded practice exams, but no instructor lectures, generally yielded a better grasp of biology and higher grades for all students, relative to courses grounded in lectures and small-group exercises.” Note the contrast between small-group exercises and peer instruction here, which is where a lot of the interesting questions are. May have to head down to the library for this one.
