# A note on LibGen and the unauthorized use of our authors' work

## Full text

Anyone following the conversation around AI and large language models (LLMs) will find Alex Reisner's article "The Unbelievable Scale of AI's Pirated-Books Problem," published yesterday in The Atlantic, eye-opening. As a publisher committed to protecting authors' rights and intellectual property, the widespread unauthorized use of MIT Press works in training data sets is deeply troubling to us.

We want to be clear: The MIT Press has not licensed any of our books or journal articles for LLM training purposes, nor have we granted permission for any such use. However, we are well aware that many MIT Press publications have ended up in pirated training data sets. We share the deep distress of our authors whose work has been made available via LibGen without their—or our—consent and has now likely been used to train commercial LLMs. Like many publishers and copyright holders, the MIT Press regularly issues take-down notices to pirate sites like LibGen, but unfortunately, these demands often go ignored. As Reisner explains, successful legal action on the part of the publishing community has so far failed to shut down this and other pirate sites or slow their growth, much of which comes from user-uploaded content.

We encourage any authors with concerns about their work to reach out to our Author Relations inbox (authinfo@mit.edu) and we will be in touch.

Amy Brand, PhD
Director and Publisher
The MIT Press
