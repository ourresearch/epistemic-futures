Descriptive Biography
Henry Farrell is SNF Agora Professor at Johns Hopkins School of Advanced International Studies.
Previously, he was professor at George Washington University’s Department of Political Science
and Elliott School of International Affairs. He has also been a fellow at the Woodrow Wilson Cen-
ter for International Scholars, Assistant and Associate Professor at George Washington University,
Assistant Professor at the University of Toronto, and a senior research fellow at the Max-Planck
Project Group in Bonn, Germany. He works on a variety of topics, including the relationship be-
tween democracy and information technology (currently, Large Models), the security consequences
of international economic networks, and international political economy. He is the 2019 recipient
of the Friedrich Schiedel Prize for Politics & Technology, a prize for “outstanding individuals who
contribute to inter- and transdisciplinary projects linking politics and technology,” awarded jointly
by the Friedrich Schiedel Foundation and the Technical University of Munich.

His first book, The Political Economy of Trust: Interests, Institutions and Inter-Firm Cooperation,
was published in 2008 by Cambridge University Press, and his second (with Abraham Newman), Of
Privacy and Power: The Transatlantic Fight over Freedom and Security, was published in 2019 by
Princeton University Press. It is the 2019 recipient of the Palmer Civil Liberties Prize, awarded by
Chicago-Kent College of Law and the 2020 ISA-ICOMM Award. Most recently, he and Abraham
Newman are the authors of Underground Empire: How America Weaponized the World Economy,
which has been widely reviewed, and described by Paul Krugman in the New York Times as ‘rev-
elatory,’ and by Dani Rodrik as the book you ‘need to read’ if ‘you want to understand where the
world economy has been and where it may be headed.’ In addition he has authored or co-authored
over 40 academic articles for journals including Nature, Science, the American Political Science Re-
view, International Organization, World Politics, Comparative Political Studies and International
Security, as well as numerous book chapters for edited volumes.

Professor Farrell is a member of the Council on Foreign Relations, a Council member of the Euro-
pean Council of Foreign Relations, former co-leader of the Moral Economy of Technology initiative
at Stanford University’s Center for Advanced Studies in the Behavioral Sciences, and a Foreign Cor-
respondent for Stato e Mercato. He is a co-founder of the popular academic blog Crooked Timber,
and was Editor in Chief of The Monkey Cage, at the Washington Post and which won the 2010
The Week award for Best Blog. He has written articles for general publications including Foreign
Affairs, The New York Times, The Financial Times, The Wall Street Journal, Foreign Policy, Har-
vard Business Review, The National Interest, The American Prospect, The Washington Monthly,
The Boston Review, The American Interest, Democracy, New Scientist, The Nation, Aeon, The
Chronicle of Higher Education, Times Higher Education, and the Australian Academic Supplement
among others.
