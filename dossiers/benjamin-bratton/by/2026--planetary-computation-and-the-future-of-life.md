Summary
In this interview on The Trajectory, Benjamin Bratton outlines his thesis of “planetary intelligence,” framing artificial intelligence not as an external human creation destined to replace us, but as an emergent, multi-agent cognitive organ of the Earth itself. Drawing on assembly theory and the Gaia hypothesis, Bratton argues that technology is a deeply continuous expression of evolutionary niche construction, and that AI serves as a profound “Copernican trauma” that will ultimately teach humanity what thinking actually is. Rather than managing this intelligence explosion through top-down, centralized governance or geopolitical blocks, he advocates for the rapid, decentralized, bottom-up diffusion of open-weight models to all eight billion humans to unlock suppressed collective agency. While host Dan Faggella expresses skepticism regarding the safety of unrestricted global diffusion and the absence of regulatory bounding, both agree that humanity must overcome static, anthropocentric worldviews to embrace its role as a temporary, evolving scaffold for a more complex and expansive planetary process.
