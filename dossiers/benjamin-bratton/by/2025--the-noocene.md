# The Noocene: Computation and Cosmology from Antikythera to AI

## Full text

The evolution of thought is a terraforming force. Now that the performance of cognition is achievable by mineral substrates, that dynamic is radicalized. How can humanity's preeminent position of sapience —in all its global and historical distribution— make conceptual sense of that phase transition?

The “Noosphere” is the sphere of thought that emerges from the geophysical and biological spheres of Earth through the interaction of minds. It is not metaphysical; it is the cumulative result of matter becoming cognitive, and partially aware of itself. Crucially, the evolution of intelligence at planetary scale is also the cause of a comprehensive artificialization of Earth: the Noocene.

This epoch is defined by a paradox. At the same historical moment that complex intelligence begins to grasp its own evolution, it also begins to recognize that its success may undermine the foundations of its future. What is the role of computation and cosmology in both recognitions? How might this epoch come to better understand itself and thus orient itself?

At least as far back as the Antikythera mechanism in 200 BC, computation has been a tool of cosmology. That mechanism was both a calculative and navigational tool, and like computation today, it oriented intelligence in relation to its planetary condition. Despite significantly clearer and more precise astronomical awareness, our grasp of what computation implies for the composition of the next Earth remains cloudy and contradictory.

Is this confusion due to a lingering misrecognition of how the evolution of intelligence, including our own, is a planetary phenomenon?
As complex intelligence transforms the planet in its own image, the Noocene, how must it orient its own future?
Beginning at least two millennia ago, how has computation revealed our planetary condition to us, from heliocentrism to climate science?
How does the artificialization of intelligence force us to correct our view by rediscovering what intelligence even is?
Computation is not simply our computers, but more profoundly how information structures the universe in time. How else may computation be directed as a technology of knowledge and composition?
What may come from a renewed commitment to cosmology in an open and expansive astronomic sense, as opposed to cultural cosmology in a closed and anthropocentric sense?
If complex intelligence, including our own, were to understand itself in this way, can it conceive and compose a future worth living through?

***

The question is now how deliberate, composed, and artificial those shifts may be. Previous epochal shifts were brought about by complex dynamical relations between biological and geological forces, all of which are arguably intelligent in some meaningful albeit massively distributed way, but while each local action may have been purposeful, the cumulative macrotransformation was not.

In the human era, however, intelligence plays a much more agential role, though one that is never omniscient nor even capable of fully grasping the cascading consequences of its own action or inaction. The abrupt artificialization of the Earth known as the “anthropocene,” a now slightly passé term but with still much to teach us, is, I argue less the result of the industry of a particular species as such, or a particular economic system favored by it in recent years, than the evolution of complex cognition itself.

The Noocene is the planetary epoch defined by the willful agency of mind. It is what happens when a planet produces not only complex life but a complex intelligent life that is successful in reproducing itself because it is successful in artificializing its environments.

Posed directly, regardless of who or how it happened, once a planet brings forth sapient life capable of collective abstraction, symbolization and mechanization, is it inevitable that this life will terraform the planet in its image? We have only one example to consider, our own, and so it is impossible to compare alternative timelines. We look forward with similar uncertainty but with some awareness. Traversing the course between path dependency and contingency, decision and evolution, the next Earth coheres with or without us, but most likely through us and through myriad technologies to which we are inextricably attached.

Seen this way, artificialization is not the opposite of the evolved, it is the project of evolution. Evolution selects for those who become expert artificialization their environments, such as the precocious fire apes. The transformation runs in both directions. In remaking the world in its image, intelligent life remakes itself. Evolutionary biology calls this niche construction, the universal process by which life doesn’t merely adapt to an ecological niche but alters it in such a way that doing so allows it to capture more energy and matter, producing more information. Doing so allows the population to not just persist but also to scale. To get good at this project, that species must become intelligent in order to develop effective techniques, some of extraordinary complexity.

Crucially, the cycle is recursive. The lifeform gets so good at altering its niche to its benefit that it is now a different niche than the one to which it first adapted and so the selection pressures forced back upon that species are different too. In turn the species adapts to new demands that it has indirectly brought about. It artificializes itself by artificializing its world and vice versa. The whole process is prone to runaway effects—such is the story of homo sapiens. For better or worse, waves of primate colonies became so good at artificializing their local niches that they grew unique brain and body characteristics to adapt to the collective requirements of their own anthropogenic project. In time, they made the entire planet their niche. That brings us up to date.

The perhaps slightly counterintuitive lesson is that “humans” specifically are not by necessity the core agents of this cycle; we appear to be the protagonists but more precisely we are the medium through the evolution of complex intelligence terraformed Earth. We are the vehicle of the Noocene but not its master. Perhaps if the big meteor had missed Earth instead of wiping out the dinosaurs it could have been someone else, or perhaps not. Perhaps the path dependency was set in motion already once mammals appeared. In this sense, humans are exceptional in that so much sapient agency is concentrated in our minds, cultural initiatives and artificial environments, but we are not exceptional in the sense that this puts us above or separate from planetary evolutionary and ecological realities. To the contrary, our provisional exceptionality is something that those very processes have brought about. We are their accomplishments as much as they are ours.

In the future, it is not unlikely that complex creative and constructive intelligence operates through an additional medium, perhaps not even biological and perhaps one of our own making. If so, what kind of niche construction and self-artificialization is this? What is a species that artificializes its world in such a way that it artificializes the very process that drives successful artificialization, intelligence itself? Is this an advanced Noocene, or something else?

***

Ideas about The Noocene are both ancient and new. They link the present moment at which artificial intelligence emerges – “the fire apes have made the rocks think fast” – with the deep evolutionary history of human intelligence, computational intelligence and planetary intelligence, which are, in the end, all part of the same story.

Critical artifacts tell a story about the conjunction of computation and cosmology and how philosophy can resolve that conjunction or mystify it. Cosmology, in the astronomical sense, and cosmology, in the anthropological sense, are fatally divergent. At present, computation represents a fundamental challenge not because of its failure, but because of its successes. A new synthesis between cosmology and cosmology is essential.

Harmonia Macrocosmica

Harmonia Macrocosmica was the definitive atlas of astronomy in the post-Copernican era, written by Andreas Cellarius and published by Johannes Janssonius in Amsterdam. This original edition from 1662 organized then comprehensive knowledge of astronomy, earth science, cartography, and related philosophy, illustrated with hundreds of breathtaking engravings, mostly anonymously composed.

In establishing post-Copernican astronomy as a fully-formed cosmology, the atlas’ publication was an important moment in a lineage that connects calculative technologies with planetary science and philosophy, from the antikythera mechanism to artificial intelligence. Its ambition toward synthetic knowledge, for which parts are known in relation to the wholes in which they are situated, was, as always, provisional but profound.

Here is a priceless example of philosophy reconciling the ontological challenges of what calculation reveals about the world, embracing the technological alienation of our intuitive perception of the horizon as the basis of a more profound cosmology.

Lunar Orbiter 1

Overshadowed by the more famous Earthrise image from 1968, the Lunar Orbiter 1 image from 1966 is the first image of Earth rising, taken from the perspective of Luna, our single moon. The image was splashed across the front page of newspapers all over the world and inspired shock, awe and wonder. Presented here is one of what are believed to be no more than 9 original prints made directly upon receipt back on Earth.

The unmanned orbiters had their own cameras and film processing units inside, taking up a third of the size of the craft. Using two lenses, the system took the pictures, developed and processed them, scanned them and transmitted the data back to Earth where they were recorded to analog tape. This is one of seven original prints made by engineers at Boeing in Seattle WA on August 23, 1966 upon receipt of signals from the Moon.

Here is a new challenge to thought. An artifact from the outside, this image of allocentric insight would, for some, turn the world inside out.

Der Spiegel

Sometimes Philosophy is scared of astronomy.

In his famous interview with Der Spiegel, the notorious philosopher Martin Heidegger was asked many questions, including for the first time in the public press about his deep involvement with the Nazi Party during the 1930s. On this topic he was generally evasive, but was then shown a copy of the Lunar Orbiter 1 image which provoked a far stronger reaction. He shuddered, ‘We do not need atomic bombs, the uprooting of man is already here. We only have pure technical relations. It is no longer an Earth on which humans live today.’ This is philosophy refusing to become cosmology.

He realized that the “world” that “Man” had inhabited up until now –flat, inert, predictable, full, reassuring- was forever gone. There was simply no way to see it again or to inhabit it again. That world was indeed destroyed. For Heidegger this was an end but for others, ourselves included, it was a beginning. In stark contrast with his lament, perhaps it is only with perspectives such as these, technically mediated and disorienting, that humans can finally live on the Earth as it is, not as we prefer to imagine it. This is the basis of any viable cosmology.

The Monolith

What does the next synthesis look like?

The Antikythera Monolith recalls the black obelisk from 2001: A Space Odyssey, a beacon that called out to HAL9000, an early AI which reluctantly brought astronauts on its journey to answer the aliens’ call. The Monolith features selections from Antikythera’s archive presented as a generative matrix of cinematic source materials overlaid with key concepts emerging from the reorientation of planetary computation. It is a movie to be read and an architectural surface to be absorbed.

The Monolith is not presented as a work of art, nor even primarily of design, but as a physical mechanism for the compressed presentation of ideas, to be both seen and read, both scanned and contemplated. Humans have now learned to produce and ingest information at scales and at speeds that would frighten our ancestors. This new literacy is based on information abundance rather than information scarcity.

The Monolith is Antikythera’s sketch for a new computational cosmology, one for which philosophy embraces the alienation as the priceless gift that it is.

***

Let us unwind the Noocene.

Slowly and then suddenly, the Blue Marble grew a crust, a sensory layer. Cities, satellites, and supercomputers are part of an evolving technosphere that expands the functional diameter of the planet. This is more than a global tool, it is an armature of cognition that has evolved over the last century.

Zooming in, planetary computation is seen as a single interlocking stack, an accidental megastructure. It is as big as the globe's surface, and extending into Low Earth Orbit, and it is as small as 3 nanometers, the size of the logic gate on advanced chips.

As of this moment the total utilized computational capacity of planetary computation is roughly 1 zettaFLOPS, or 1021 floating point operations per second. That is 1 Planetary Computing Unit. 1 PCU = 1 zettaFLOPS.

Over the last decades that total has doubled every 30 months. If that holds then it is 2.5 years to 2 PCU, 6 years to 10 PCU and 16 years to 100 PCU, which would be a world as unfamiliar to us as 1 PCU is to your grandparents, and yet most of us will soon live there.

What are the lessons to be learned in advance from this arc? How should it be located in the geophysical history of its host planet? What does it mean for that planet to artificialize its preferred engine of information cognition?

The most durable lesson is the protean nature of the ground itself, unfixed and unfixable. The Earth terraforms itself over and over and will do so now and into the future. The present Earth is the result of past unintentional terraformings—such as the mass extinction events that produced our atmosphere—and its present form is the precondition of equally profound metamorphoses in the future.
