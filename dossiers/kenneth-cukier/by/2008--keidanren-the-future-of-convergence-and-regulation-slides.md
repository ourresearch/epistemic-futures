The new TV: anything, anytime, anywhere

EU “Audiovisual Media Services”

Problem: Rules Without Borders

- Approved in 2007 to take affect by 2010. Meant to modernize the
“Television Without Frontiers” directives of 1989 and 1997.

- Anyone can be a “TV station” today, just as anyone
could be a “publisher” in 1455, but new rules carry
over a “licensing” approach.

- Facilitates cross-border market entrance by harmonizing rules.
- Controls content, programming times and advertising amounts.
- Distinguishes ‘linear’ (broadcast TV-like) and ‘non-linear’ (web-like)
content, but entails a large gray-zone of uncertainty.

- Public broadcasters, feeling threatened, sought to define
“linear” vs “non-linear” content so as to place the
same regulatory burdens on new online content
creators (programming, advertising limits, etc).
- Incumbent broadcasters did not appreciate that easing
the rules would benefit them greatly -- since they have
professional content and an established audience.

2. US ‘Network Neutrality’ Legislation

US Net Neutrality Proposals
- Attempts to ensure that the basic transmission function
of the Internet remains “open” -- that it, not impeded
by network operators.
- Prevents network operators from blocking or charging
more for carrying certain types of traffic -- whether due
to the large volume, or because the senders’ business
undermines the operators’ commercial interests.

Only one economic model for the net?!

Problems: Economic Subjectivity
- Solidifies a single economic model for a medium that is
typified by change.
- Classic tools of regulatory policy (eg, interconnection,
non-discrimination, market dominance, etc) are better.
- Regulatory over-reach: the issue is scarcity of access
and transmission, which can be addressed in itself.

How un-Internet-like to centralize the system!

Lessons from EU and US Mistakes
- Companies need to embrace convergence and
regulation to adapt their businesses -- the sooner they
do so, the stronger they become.

Conclusion
- Regulators need to use the market to “regulate”
activities rather than rely on policy (which otherwise
risks pouring concrete on an industry structure).

Convergence will bring success to many

A chance to tidy the regulatory mess

The next “convergence”: ubiquitous networks

Ultimately

-- Business models are in flux, and should
be allowed to emerge and evolve to benefit
the public and the economy
