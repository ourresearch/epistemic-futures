## Quick bio

Kenneth Neil Cukier (b. 1968) is **Deputy Executive Editor of The Economist**, after two decades at the paper as technology correspondent (London), Tokyo correspondent (2007–2012), data editor, and commentary editor; he was also managing editor of the paper's 2018 "Open Future" project and hosted/co-hosted its weekly science-and-technology podcast **Babbage**. Earlier he was technology editor of the Wall Street Journal Asia in Hong Kong and worked at the International Herald Tribune in Paris. He is co-author of **Big Data: A Revolution That Will Transform How We Live, Work, and Think** (with Viktor Mayer-Schönberger, 2013 — NYT bestseller, 20+ translations, FT/McKinsey Business Book of the Year finalist), **Learning with Big Data** (2014), and **Framers: Human Advantage in an Age of Technology and Turmoil** (with Mayer-Schönberger and Francis de Véricourt, Dutton 2021). He was a research fellow at Harvard's Kennedy School (2002–04) and an associate fellow at Oxford's Saïd Business School (2016–24), and he was a Chatham House trustee/board director (dates given variously as 2016–22 and 2018–24 by different self-authored sources); **both the Saïd fellowship and the Chatham House seat have ended** — his own institutional bios now put them in the past tense. He is a member of the Council on Foreign Relations and a board director of the European Graduate School. He is a Quaker who attends Richmond Meeting in England, teaches a course on AI and religion at the Woodbrooke Centre, advises AIandFaith.org, and is **writing a solo book on spirituality and AI**.
