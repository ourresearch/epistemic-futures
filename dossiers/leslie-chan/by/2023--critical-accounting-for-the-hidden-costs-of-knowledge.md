“Counter accounts aim to challenge and critique accounts produced
by hegemonic groups, exposing their partiality, contradictions, and
deceptions by giving voice to the marginalised and laying bare
previously unseen abuses and ugly excesses of corporate and state
power (Apostol, 2015). By demystifying and unmasking the dark,
messy reality that lies beneath the beautified spectacle of corporate
communications and reporting (Boiral, 2013), counter accounts aim
to generate “moral outrage” (Himick & Ruff, 2019) about various
forms of social, economic, and environmental injustice, and in so
doing catalyse transformative political action (Vinnari & Laine,
2017).”
Tweedie, J. (2022). The emancipatory potential of counter

Platform Capitalism
• Economic and business models centered around
integrated digital systems that facilitate multi-sided
transactions, interactions, and exchanges between
different user groups.
• Companies create and operate digital platforms as
intermediaries, connecting buyers and sellers, service
providers and customers, or content creators and
audiences. These platforms leverage technology, data,
and network effects to dominate various industries and
disrupt traditional business models.
Key characteristics
• Intermediation – become indispensable in interactions
and decision making
• Network Effects - the value of the platform increases as
the number of users grows, this facilitate lock-in and
high switching costs
• Big Data and Constant Surveillance - collect and analyze
vast amounts of user data generated through platform
interactions. This data is used to personalize services
and optimize business operations.
• Many private platforms are becoming essential
public Infrastructure
