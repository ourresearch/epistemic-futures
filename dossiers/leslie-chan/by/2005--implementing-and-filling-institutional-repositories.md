What
a set of services that a university offers to the members of its community for the management and dissemination of digital materials created by the institution and its community members. It is most essentially an organizational commitment to the stewardship of these digital materials, including long-term preservation where appropriate, as well as organization and access or distribution.”
Clifford A. Lynch, "Institutional Repositories: Essential Infrastructure for Scholarship in the Digital Age" ARL, no. 226 (February 2003): 1-7.

MITs experience was that faculty did not submit their own materials. Departmental or person administrative staff did. At the U of T, adding this task, however small, to an already diminished cadre of admin staff has been a key barrier to populating the database – it simply fell to the bottom of the “to do pile” and did not get done. We piloted, the use of student assistants, w/ 1 early adopter and then expanded the program. The library will assist in hiring, training and ongoing troubleshooting. Student assistants verify citations, check self archiving clauses, submit and digitze materials.

John Kennedy’s 1974 book: A Psychology of Picture Perception was copyright cleared, digitized and mounted in T-Space on Feb 19,2004. In just over a year, the e-book has had 4,177 hits. In contrast Prof. Kennedy’s online CV has been hit approx. 1681 in the same period. Each chapter has been visited an average of 229 times, ranging from 117 to 521 visitors - the 6 print copies held by U of T Libraries have circulated only once since Feb 19, 2004 and a total of 57 times since 1992. 
Robin Elliot’s previously unpublished conference paper Sounds Canadian: Music, History and Music History in Taptoo! has been downloaded 447 times since being archived on Jan. 8, 2004.
In late 2002, Rudy Boonstra co-authored an article The utility of Ki-67 and BrdU as proliferative markers of adult neurogenesis . Since being posted on September 11, 2003, citations have risen: 3 in 2002, 11 in 2003, 29 in 2004 and 5 in 2005. 

Plus GoogleScholar

The fact that that their documents will not only get priority indexing, but ranking in Google and now GoogleScholar is a key selling point w/ many faculty.

Content Policy: Who decides?
Who can submit content? Faculty, research affiliates, students?
Must the work be education or research-oriented?
Will the repository accept peer-reviewed content only?
Does the work have to be born digital?
Does the work have to be in finished form, ready for distribution?
Does the author/owner have to grant the service the right to preserve and distribute the content?
If the work is part of a series, must other works in that series be contributed as well?

Adapted from Barton and Waters, 2004 http://dspace.org/implement/leadirs.pdf

Content Policy : Organization
How will your collections be organised e.g. by Department, Subject, or Document Type?
What constitutes a collection?

What are your contingency plans if a department ceases to exist?

Submission and Access Policy
Is there an approval process for content being submitted?
Are submitters notified of an item’s progress in the submission process?
Are there content size limits for individual items, individual faculty members, or collections?
Will you have a user agreement with end-users of the system?
Will you institute a privacy policy for those who register with the system?
Will you allow limited access to certain items?
