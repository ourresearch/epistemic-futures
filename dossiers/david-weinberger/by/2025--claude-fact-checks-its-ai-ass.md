# Claude fact-checks its AI ass

## Full text

A minute ago I posted a post that Claude, the excellent AI chat bot , wrote for me. It’s about Nautical Twilight. That apparently is a real thing at least.

I just asked it to fact check what it wrote. Here’s the transcript:

Claude writes:

Let me fact-check my blog post about nautical twilight:

Accurate information:

Nautical twilight is defined as when the sun is between 6 and 12 degrees below the horizon

During nautical twilight, the horizon remains visible while bright stars appear

This combination made it ideal for celestial navigation

Celestial navigation traditionally used a sextant to measure angles between celestial bodies and the horizon

The term “nautical” relates to its usefulness for navigation

Many marine organisms do change their behavior during twilight periods (diel vertical migration)

The U.S. Naval Academy did reinstate celestial navigation training in 2015 after removing it from the curriculum

Potentially misleading or requiring clarification:

I didn’t specify that nautical twilight happens twice daily – both before sunrise (morning nautical twilight) and after sunset (evening nautical twilight)

Photographers often call the period shortly after sunset/before sunrise the “blue hour,” but this typically overlaps with civil twilight more than nautical twilight

While some marine species do time migrations with light changes, I should’ve been more specific about which species and behaviors

Missing context:

I didn’t mention that nautical twilight duration varies significantly by latitude and season

I didn’t explain that nautical almanacs contain pre-calculated data for celestial navigation

I didn’t clarify that while celestial navigation is still taught, GPS and other electronic navigation systems are the primary methods used today

Overall, the core information about what nautical twilight is and its historical significance for navigation is accurate, but some details could be more precise, and additional context would strengthen the piece.
