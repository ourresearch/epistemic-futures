# The information war paradox

## Abstract (only openly available portion)

This chapter looks at the concept of 'information war' through the example of the Kremlin's operations in Ukraine. It warns that the adoption of the term by Russian state-aligned actors as a form of propaganda has created a paradox whereby it is necessary to expose the Kremlin's use of 'information war' operations, while to do so risks reinforcing the Kremlin's world-view. This 'information war' frame renders facts coincidental and critical engagement impossible. Information war is closely linked to military warfare; the Kremlin undermines the Ukrainian narrative of fighting a clear enemy by waging war without openly declaring it, and Russian military intervention is conducted with its effect on the information conflict in mind. An effective Ukrainian response cannot be top-down, orchestrated by senior government officials or editors of traditional media, but will require the participation of Ukrainians at every level of society.
