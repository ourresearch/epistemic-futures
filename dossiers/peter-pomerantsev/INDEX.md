## Quick bio

Peter Pomerantsev (b. 1977, Kyiv) is a journalist and author who writes about propaganda,
disinformation and the politics of information. He is a Senior Fellow at the SNF Agora Institute
at Johns Hopkins University, where he co-directs the Arena Initiative
([SNF Agora profile](https://snfagora.jhu.edu/person/peter-pomerantsev/)); he directed Arena at
the LSE Institute of Global Affairs from 2017 to 2020, and before that led the "Beyond Propaganda"
programme at the Legatum Institute's Transitions Forum and chaired CEPA's Information Warfare
Initiative. He lived and worked in Russian television in Moscow between roughly 2001 and 2010,
which became the material for his first book. He has written three books —
*Nothing Is True and Everything Is Possible* (2014), *This Is Not Propaganda* (2019) and
*How to Win an Information War* (2024) — and co-hosts *Autocracy in America* with Anne Applebaum
for The Atlantic. He has testified on information warfare to the US House Foreign Affairs
Committee, the US Senate Foreign Relations Committee and the US Helsinki Commission, and was a
specialist adviser to the UK parliamentary inquiry into "fake news."

**Name spelling:** the standard spelling is **Pomerantsev**. "Pomerantzsev" appears in some event
materials and "Pomeranzev" in some institutional pages (including his own Coda Story author slug,
`/author/peterpomeranzev/`) — those are variants of the same person.

**Disambiguation:** his father is **Igor Pomerantsev**, a poet and RFE/RL and BBC World Service
broadcaster; their one co-authored item ("The distorting mirror," a father–son conversation) is
included and labelled as such, but Igor's own work is not. Also excluded: Alexey L. Pomerantsev
(chemometrics, RAS), Eugene V. Pomerantsev (interventional cardiology, Stanford), V. N. Pomerantsev
(nuclear physics) and A. N. Pomerantsev (architecture) — all distinct people who surface on a
surname search.

### Method notes worth reusing

- The LRB contributor page paginates with a plain `?page=N` query parameter even though the
  listing looks JavaScript-driven; 14 pages of HTML gave a complete 65-item enumeration.
- `r.jina.ai/<url>` was the workhorse for Cloudflare-walled publishers (Granta, Hachette,
  Kirkus, Foreign Policy, LA Review of Books, SNF Agora, TED). For WordPress sites it is worth
  following up with `/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?slug=…`, which returns much cleaner body HTML.
- `api.govinfo.gov/search` with `DEMO_KEY` is a fast, free way to find congressional testimony
  by witness surname.
- **Wayback CDX domain-wide queries with a regex filter** (`matchType=domain` +
  `filter=original:.*pomerantsev.*`) reliably 504 on medium-sized news domains even at 400–550s
  timeouts. Exact-URL and short-prefix CDX lookups return in 5–40s. The one domain-wide query
  that *did* work was mime-type-filtered rather than regex-filtered
  (`url=li.com&matchType=domain&filter=mimetype:application/pdf`).
- **Search substitute with WebSearch exhausted**: `r.jina.ai/https://lite.duckduckgo.com/lite/?q=…`
  returns clean result links (plain `html.duckduckgo.com` over `curl` returns a 202 anomaly page).
  Driving `html.duckduckgo.com/html/?q=…` through `claude-in-chrome` also works well; short,
  unquoted queries return results where long boolean ones return nothing.
- **`r.jina.ai` issues rate-limit domain bans mid-run** (`www.nytimes.com` was blocked for ~75
  minutes during this pass, newstatesman.com's wp-json after ~20 calls). Wayback covered both.

- **Wayback CDX prefix queries are a URL oracle**: `cdx?url=prospectmagazine.co.uk/culture/47128*`
  returned the one canonical Prospect URL in seconds, which is what made the live fetch possible.
  Same trick failed on `washingtonpost.com/style/media/2025/08/06*` — a prefix only works when you
  can guess the section path.
- **LARB serves plain `curl`** with a browser User-Agent and no paywall; `r.jina.ai` is not needed
  for it (and is now 403-gated anyway).
