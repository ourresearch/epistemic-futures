# Sewing Patterns over iTunes

## Full text

Back at our first P2P conference in 2001, one of the most thought-provoking talks was entitled [Napster Fabbing](http://www.ennex.com/~fabbers/publish/200102-Napster.asp). Marshall Burns and James Howison posited that with the rise of [personal fabrication](http://www.amazon.com/exec/obidos/tg/detail/-/0465027458/timsarchiv-20), we could look forward to a future in which not just songs but _stuff_ would be shared over p2p networks. After all, in a world of digitally-driven fabrication machines, what is stuff but a set of instructions?(Biology works that way already.)

We're not there yet for digital fabrication, but in classic [news from the future](http://www.makezine.com/nff/) form, we're seeing headlines that show us moving bit-by-bit in that direction. One such headline came from Phil Torrone on the [Make: blog](http://www.makezine.com/blog), who reported on [the first sewing pattern delivered by iTunes](http://www.makezine.com/blog/archive/2006/08/sewing_patterns_over_itunes_rs.html?CMP=OTC-0D6B48984890).

  
Phil waggishly noted: "ok, we got our CRAFT itunes thing going - at 11:19pm PDT the first sewing pattern over itunes was delivered a "Stretch boob tube with drawstring bottom. Hipster shorts with scoop sides." - and i'm ok with this being how i spend my friday nights. so yes, we used [a series of tubes](http://radar.oreilly.com/archives/2006/06/and_these_guys_are_writing_our.html), to deliver a tube top"

(And actually, Make: has been delivering hardware designs as RSS enclosures for iTunes for a while. They're not yet for personal fab labs, just for human consumption, but the trend is coming into focus...)
