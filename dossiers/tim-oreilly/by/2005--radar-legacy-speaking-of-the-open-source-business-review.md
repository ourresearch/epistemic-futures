# Speaking of the Open Source Business Review

## Full text

I was just looking at [the program](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/pub/w/38/osbr.html), and there are a lot of interesting sessions that demonstrate just how far open source has come from its hacker roots, and what a different set of issues enterprises think about when coming to grips with open source software. Here's a sampling: 

> [Leveraging Open Source for SOX Compliance](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/cs/os2005/view/e_sess/7118) (SOX = Sarbanes Oxley, the bane of every IT department today!)   
>  [How to Safely Participate in an Open Source Project ](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/cs/os2005/view/e_sess/7483)   
>  [Open Source Licensing Trends](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/cs/os2005/view/e_sess/7144)   
>  [Eight Open Source Applications for IT You�ve Never Heard Of ](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/cs/os2005/view/e_sess/7146)   
>  [Evaluating the Risk and Maturity of Open Source Projects](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/cs/os2005/view/e_sess/7125)   
>  [Where and When Can a Linux Desktop Work?](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/cs/os2005/view/e_sess/7126)   
>  [Open Source Skill Building](http://conferences.oreillynet.com/cs/os2005/view/e_sess/7122)

Nice to see how the conference is keeping up with what is now a pretty broad user spectrum, from the bleeding edge that has been its hallmark since we started the conference in 1997 to what is rapidly becoming the mainstream.
