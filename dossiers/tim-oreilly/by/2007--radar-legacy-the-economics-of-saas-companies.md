# The Economics of SaaS Companies

## Full text

[Will Price](http://www.blogger.com/profile/07526077009135142958) of [Hummer Winblad](http://www.humwin.com) did some interesting [analysis of the relative economics](http://willprice.blogspot.com/2007/01/economics-of-saas-we-need-platform.html) of 1990s software businesses and the current generation of Software as a Service businesses:

> While the SaaS companies grew up in a different IPO market, the results suggest that SaaS companies take: 
> 
>   * 1.6x longer to get liquid 
>   * 3.65x more capital 
>   * 1.75x more revenue to hit profitability 
>   * Salesforce, for example, raised $64.52m in equity, to Peoplesoft's $10. Websidestory raised $43m to BOBJ's $5m.
> 

> 
> Click [HERE](http://i38.photobucket.com/albums/e116/pricewill/saas.png) to see data behind analysis

Now the conventional Web 2.0 wisdom is that it [costs less to launch a new software business](http://bnoopy.typepad.com/bnoopy/2005/06/its_a_great_tim.html), and there's a lot of data to support that position. What Will's analysis suggests, though, is that it costs more to get those businesses to IPO-scale profitability. This divide suggests, along with all the anecdotal data, that acquisition rather than IPO is the more likely exit for many Web 2.0 companies.
