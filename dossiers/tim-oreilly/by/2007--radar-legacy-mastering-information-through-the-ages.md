# Mastering Information Through the Ages

## Full text

As many of you know, I'm a big fan of the [Long Now Foundation](http://www.longnow.org)'s [Seminars on Long Term Thinking](http://www.longnow.org/projects/seminars). Tonight's seminar looks like a good one. Stewart Brand sent out the following writeup: 

> In a classic case of reperceiving the present via a very-long-now perspective, Alex Wright invokes examples from evolutionary biology, cultural anthropology, mythology, mysticism, the history of printing, scientific method, 18th-century taxonomy, Victorian librarians, and early computer history to get a fresh grip on what the hell is going on with information these days. Past mastery and future mastery, he finds, involves crafting the right byplay between bottom-up distributed self-organization and structural hierarchy. 
> 
> Kevin Kelly says of Wright's new book, [Glut: Mastering Information Through the Ages](http://www.amazon.com/Glut-Mastering-Information-Through-Ages/dp/0309102383/), "I found Alex Wright's quick, clear history of past methods for managing oceans of information to be a handy clue to where we are going. He introduces you to an ecosystem of information organizations far more complex and interesting than the mere 'search' tool." 
> 
> "The Deep History of the Information Age," Alex Wright, [Cowell Theater, Fort Mason](http://www.fortmason.org/directions/index.shtml), San Francisco, 7pm, Friday, August 17. The lecture starts promptly at 7:30pm. Admission is free (a $10 donation is always welcome, not required). 

While only Bay Area readers are likely to be able to attend, video of all seminars is available to [Long Now subscribers](https://secure.longnow.org/members/), usually within a few weeks after each seminar.
