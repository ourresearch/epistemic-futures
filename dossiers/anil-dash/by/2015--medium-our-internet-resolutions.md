# Our Internet Resolutions

I made internet resolutions this year.

In January I break down the segments of my life that I want to inspect and maybe improve.

_Work, hobby, social._

_Mind, body, spirit._

_Partner, family, friends._

_Town, state, world._

_Inner, outer, cosmic._

It’s -15° outside. What else is there to do but put things into boxes then unpack and rearrange them? And type. If I want to make material changes that affect my whole life, I need to do something about the internet. The 2015 split is simpler than in previous years.

**_Offline. Online._**

My resolution-type decisions each year all hinge on some logging or communicating component anyhow. Tracking is digital. Intentions are broadcast. Tools are networked.

Getting in shape? FitBit or My Fitness Pal organizes your exercise. Keeping track of reading? Goodreads or LibraryThing is your digital bookshelf. [Visiting Vermont’s 183 libraries](<http://www.sevendaysvt.com/vermont/jessamyn-west-documents-vermont-public-libraries/Content?oid=2296010>)? Wikipedia will teach you about [tiled printing](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiled_printing>) so you can create a giant wall map. BatchGeo will [dataviz the addresses](<http://www.librarian.net/stax/4209/elbow-grease-and-geocoding-making-a-map-of-vermonts-public-libraries/>) for your smartphone. Flickr stores your documentation. Your blog keeps the master list. The internet pervades. I don’t recognize my handwriting anymore.

If you want to make resolutions without crowdsourcing your compliance, keep them offline. Totally acceptable. I view other people’s resolutions as a pact with me sometimes. “Hey I thought you said you were done watching the NFL, but here you are writing a post about the game?” (This may be a mistake.)

My personal relationship with me and my body and my reading list and my obsessive list-making doesn’t need to be intermediated by a million people who don’t know me. But our relationship with the internet? We’re all in that together. It’s made of us.

> If you want to reshape the water that you don’t know is wet, make internet resolutions.

Need to change the way you interact or react in a space that is aggregating an ever-increasing chunk of your attentions and efforts? Resolve to do something about the internet.

Asking people about their resolutions can turn into a TMI exercise of regret and self-loathing, but people still love to talk about the internet. You can make party conversation about people’s internet resolutions. It’s my new “[ _Flight or invisibility_](<http://www.reddit.com/r/AskReddit/comments/l9k5t/would_you_rather_have_the_power_of_invisibility/>)?”

I have some self-improvement projects that are private, but I’m happy to talk about my internet resolutions.

### Be it resolved

  1. **Tolerating no more online harassment**. I wrapped up 2014 crabby about someone saying they wished me dead on facebook and one of the usual ankle-biters impugning my motives for just being me. No more, fuck those guys. Not going for a scorched earth approach but reporting death threats, demanding accountability from the “They’re just joking” crowd, using tools, following up, never pulling a post because I am afraid of the backlash.
  2. **An end to armchair activism**. Signing an online petition or hair-splitting arguing with internet randos is not doing the actual work of changing the world. It rarely hurts, but it can give a false sense of having done something when I’ve done nothing. Hot-potatoing good ideas around the internet should be just a start, not the whole plan.
  3. **The Jessamyn-Meshmachine project**. Make all the online versions of me into a coherent whole. Easy to say, tough to do without crosspost spamming everyplace. A work in progress.

### The Message 2015

I’m new to The Message. I asked my colleagues about their internet resolutions for 2015. It helped me get to know them. And can help you get to know us.

**_Tim Carmody_**[ _@tcarmody_](</@tcarmody>)

I like my New Year’s resolutions to have three virtues: simple, achievable, and spaced out over the entire year. For instance, for 2013 and again in 2014, I resolved to keep my ass out of the hospital: no admissions, no overnight stays. And despite some close calls, I pulled it off.

Now it’s time to go one level up. In 2014, I wrote and earned less than I ever have as an adult (mostly due to continued nagging health problems). For 2015, I want to write and publish — and get _paid_ for writing and publishing— something every month. This feels very doable to me, especially since I now have a steady gig here at The Message. Now I just need to make it happen.

**_Andy Baio_** __[_@waxpancake_](</@waxpancake>)

Block with abandon. I spent far too many emotional cycles last year on people arguing with me in bad faith, diving into arguments that could never be won. At some point, I stopped arguing and started blocking. I blocked hundreds of randos who insulted me or threatened people I admire — sea lions sauntering their way into my attention — and turned the Internet into something I could love again. Never. Again.

**_Clive Thompson_** __[_@clivethompson_](</@clivethompson>)

I always only resolve to do things that I know I’ll accomplish; thus, they tend to be hedonistic. Last year I think it was “play more video games.” I think this year I’m going to resolve to “spent more time procrastinating on work by poking around aimlessly on Wikipedia.”

** _Rex Sorgatz_** __[_@rexsorgatz_](</@rexsorgatz>)

I ended up turning this thing into a full-fledged post.

> Stop envying the escapism of The Last True Hermit. I pledge to engage with people, rather than hide under the forest of irony and glibness. I pledge to stop worshiping at the altar of an escapist fantasy.

** _Virginia Heffernan_** __[_@page88_](</@page88>)

No new mobile devices, since as other have noted the iPhone 6 Plus and the iPad mini have converged and I believe the form has found its apex. The next purchase I make (maybe an Oculus? or a neurofeedback something) will have to represent a difference in kind and not degree. I say this as my iPhone 5 is probably planning to fail this afternoon.

**_Paul Ford_** __[_@ftrain_](</@ftrain>)

Last year I found myself piling on in little Twitter fussquakes. Writing “funny” tweets about various media-and-tech-industry inside-baseball nonsense. This year I will avoid saying posturing snippy things on the Internet. I may think I’m being hi-lar-ious but I’m really just another snoot (I wish there was an app that made you give $5 to charity before allowing you to tweet). I will also: Finish my book about how the web changed culture, which is late; avoid appearing on or attending panel discussions; lose the same 90 pounds I lost five years ago; and get an honest job that doesn’t involve word counts. If I can’t make that work I will at least try to become a more specific writer. Each paragraph should be about something, and defensible on its own merits.

**_Joanne McNeil_** __[_@joannemcneil_](</@joannemcneil>)

Check my spam folder once a week. So many opportunities I nearly missed because they were filtered out. A friend of mine almost lost a substantial commission because it was filtered in spam. Maybe phrasings like “You have been nominated” triggers it?

**_Zeynep Tufekci_** __[_@zeynep_](</@zeynep>)

Non-fiction — the world I live, breathe and write in — has facts on its side but fiction is where truth lies. My resolution is to dive back into fiction, but it’s not a new year’s resolution. It’s a post-tenure file resolution (due this September eeek!). This is the year I finish a book on social movements and new technology (coming out of Yale University Press, woot!), there may also be a short book in the works (a 20K work that is looking for its outlet), and some article commitments, the Message and other writings, _and_ I go up for tenure so all my time is accounted for — and some. I’ve also decided that I will set up a walking desk soon: I injured an ankle which cut into my serious athletics so this is one way of compensating, especially given how much I writing I have to do this year (aka sitting on my butt). I can’t think if I don’t move, every day, a lot. Post-tenure, I am going to read last decade of science fiction, I am going to watch good movies, and I am going to seek fiction that is in forms that did not exist when I stopped regularly reading fiction (aka when I became full time academic). That means learning how fiction lives natively on the Internet—a world I have not yet really discovered.

**_Anil Dash_** __[_@anildash_](</@anildash>)

I think my internet resolutions are (1) to keep trying to be mindful about who and what I amplify, understanding that I have been given a platform that I have to do justice to. (2) to focus more on being **effective** in the impact I have online. Outrage is fine as a starting point, but I want to hone that into concrete actions.

**_Jessamyn West_** __[_@jessamyn_](</@jessamyn>)

I believe in the internet. I’m still excited about [where it will go](<http://cluetrain.com/newclues/>). This means I treat it like a real space where I’m as accessible and accountable and authentic as I am in any other space. Join me.
