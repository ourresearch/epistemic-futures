# How to Stop an AI Arms Race

## Abstract (only openly available portion)

The US and China are locked in a high stakes race to control the future of AI. This Essay begins by arguing that an AI race is irrational, posing serious risks to both countries. The Essay then critiques existing proposals for avoiding such a race. The existing proposals are too indexed on the international nonproliferation and disarmament paradigms that worked for the last global arms race–the Cold War race for nuclear supremacy. AI technology is quite different from nuclear technology. The game-theoretic strategies that produced relative stability in the late Cold War will thus not work for de-escalating the AI arms race.

The Essay then introduces and defends a better strategy: an international agreement under which the US and China would collaborate to rapidly develop the world's most powerful, but safe, AIs. The centerpiece of our proposal is the formation of a joint AI lab that would combine the best US and Chinese AI talent and be supercharged by massive sovereign investments by both nations. We argue that this approach has far better political and game-theoretic properties than the alternatives. Compared with either an AI race or a nonproliferation strategy, the joint lab strategy would be both a safer and a faster route to AI development. For these reasons, both AI safety advocates and AI accelerationists should endorse the joint lab proposal.
