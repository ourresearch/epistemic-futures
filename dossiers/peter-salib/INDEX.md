## Quick bio

Peter N. Salib is the W. Ronald Robins Endowed Professor and an Associate Professor of Law (with
tenure) at the University of Houston Law Center, and Associated Faculty at UH's Hobby School of
Public Affairs ([UH Law Center faculty page,
2026](https://www.law.uh.edu/faculty/main.asp?PID=6428)). He is the founder and Executive
Co-Director of the Center for Law & AI Risk (CLAIR, clair-ai.org), a Law and Policy Advisor to the
Center for AI Safety, a Visiting Senior Fellow at the Institute for Law & AI, a Contributing Editor
at Lawfare, and was a Senior Visiting Scholar at Forethought (Feb–July 2026). His research sits at
the intersection of public law, economics, and artificial intelligence, and centers on "law as an
alignment technology" — extending legal rights and duties to advanced AI systems so that
rule-of-law incentives push them toward cooperation with humans. Earlier work covers criminal-law
economics (arguing against imprisonment), constitutional structure, and algorithmic discrimination.
Before entering the academy he was a Climenko Fellow at Harvard Law School, clerked for Judge Frank
H. Easterbrook (7th Cir.), and practiced appellate litigation at Sidley Austin. The UH page states
he has a book on AI rights forthcoming with Cambridge University Press; his own site dates it
*AI Rights*, with Simon Goldstein, Cambridge University Press (forthcoming 2027)
([peternsalib.com/research](https://peternsalib.com/research/)). That site also lists his current
forthcoming placements: *AI Rights for Human Safety* (Virginia Law Review 2026), *AI Suffrage for
Human Flourishing* (Fordham Law Review 2026), *How to Count AIs* (Boston College Law Review 2026)
and *AI Is Not a Natural Monopoly* (Minnesota Law Review Online 2026).

### Technique notes (for the fan-out)

- **SSRN via same-origin iframes.** Cloudflare's managed challenge fires on `fetch()`/XHR from a
  page on papers.ssrn.com, but *not* on a same-origin `<iframe>` navigation. Appending hidden
  iframes and polling `iframe.contentDocument` until the text contains "Suggested Citation" reliably
  scraped abstract pages at ~1 per 25–40 s. Two constraints: the CDP `Runtime.evaluate` timeout is
  45 s (so kick the loop off unawaited and poll for completion), and `javascript_tool` truncates
  returned strings at roughly 1 KB (so dump long results by writing them into a `<pre>` in the page
  and reading them back with `get_page_text`, which handles ~11 KB).
- **A shared browser tab really does get reaped.** A tab holding 26 scraped SSRN abstracts in a
  `window` variable was closed by another agent mid-run and the data was lost; the second attempt
  wrote results out in small batches instead. Don't accumulate state in the browser.
- **bepress / Digital Commons `viewcontent.cgi` returns HTTP 202 with a zero-byte body** to plain
  curl, and `r.jina.ai` gets a CAPTCHA page. It serves the PDF normally if you first fetch the
  article landing page into a cookie jar and then request the PDF with that jar, a `Referer` of the
  landing page, and a full browser header set (`Sec-Fetch-Dest: document`, `Sec-Fetch-Mode:
  navigate`, `Sec-Fetch-Site: same-origin`, `Upgrade-Insecure-Requests: 1`). This unlocked Michigan,
  Northwestern, William & Mary, Chicago Unbound, Duke and Tulsa.
- **pdftotext can silently return 0 characters** on Word→pdfHarmony PDFs with non-embedded fonts
  (Duke Law Journal Online). `pymupdf` extracted the same file fine. Keep it as a fallback.
- **lawfaremedia.org 403s every non-browser client**; `r.jina.ai` works, and usefully returns the
  readability-extracted article for most pages.
- **iTunes Search API** (`itunes.apple.com/search?term=…&entity=podcastEpisode`) is a free,
  no-key substitute for web search when enumerating podcast appearances, and `lookup?id=…` gives the
  raw RSS feed URL for episode dates and durations.
