# first total-impact summit

## Full text

Heather and I (Jason) have been working pretty much nonstop on [total-impact](http://total-impact.org/) since Thursday, first at her place in Vancouver, and for the last two days in between meetings and meals with the super-smart folks behind [Eigenfactor](http://www.eigenfactor.org/) and [SSRN](http://www.ssrn.com/) here in Seattle. We’ve got a few things to report. 

First, we’ve reorganized our planning infrastructure to make it simpler, more agile, and more responsive to the community. If you want to add a feature, drop it in the [requests gDoc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/16Ef_SPqLWBmiXgJUIMBbyGIFT3XEnaX5BtiiG-51YQo/edit?hl=en_US&pli=1). Done. We’ve also ditched all the roadmap docs in favor of a single, prioritized [product backlog](http://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/scrum/product-backlog) in [this gSpreadsheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheet/ccc?key=0Ai0SDlWE5_VYdEMyenZ0M1Vab0lLOWJfeDZ5REVEd0E&hl=en_US#gid=0), and we’re going to be using this blog regularly to supplement our mailing list.

Second, we’re finishing up writing spec for our next release, Jean-Claude (yep, [that one](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Van_Damme). Our name sounds like an action flick; why fight it). Our goal is to have, after six two-week sprints, a total-impact web app with the same functions it does now, but built on its own API, scaleable to tens of thousands of calls per day. We’re also re-writing the PHP bits in Python, to take better advantage of the large and growing community of scientist Pythonistas.
