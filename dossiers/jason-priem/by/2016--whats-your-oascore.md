# What’s your #OAscore?

## Full text

\
We’re all obsessed with self-measurement.

We measure how much we’re Liked online. We measure [how many steps we take in a day](https://help.fitbit.com/articles/en_US/Help_article/1143). And as academics, we measure our success using publication counts, h-indices, and even Impact Factors.

But we’re missing something.

As academics, our fundamental job is not to amass citations, but to increase the collective wisdom of our species. It’s an important job. Maybe even a sacred one. It matters. And it’s one we profoundly fail at when we lock our work behind paywalls.

Given this, there’s a measurement that must outweigh all the others we use (and [misuse](http://occamstypewriter.org/scurry/2012/08/13/sick-of-impact-factors/)) as researchers: how much of our work can be read?

This [Open Access Week](http://www.openaccessweek.org/), we’re rolling out this measurement on Impactstory. It’s a simple number: what percentage of your work is free to read online? We’d argue that it’s perhaps the most important number associated with your professional life (unless maybe it’s the percentage of your work published with a [robust license](http://sparcopen.org/our-work/howopenisit/) that allows reuse beyond reading…we’re calculating that too). We’re calling it your Open Access Score.![](https://i0.wp.com/blog.impactstory.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/ImpactStoryGraphicsFinal-300x150.png?resize=300%2C150)

We’d like to issue a challenge to every researcher: find out your open access score, do one thing to raise it, and tell someone you did. It takes ten minutes, and it’s a concrete thing you can do to be proud of yourself as a scholar.

### Here’s how to do it:

1.  [Make an Impactstory profile](http://impactstory.org/landing/open). You’ll need a Twitter account and nothing more…it’s free, nonprofit, and takes less than five minutes. Plus along the way you’ll learn cool stuff about how often your research has been tweeted, blogged, and discussed online.
2.  Deposit just one of your papers into an Open Access repository. Again: it’s easy. [Here’s instructions](http://blog.impactstory.org/impact-challenge-data-repository/).
3.  Once you’re done, update your Impactstory, and see your improved score.
4.  Tweet it. Let your community know you’ve made the world a richer, more beautiful place because you’ve made you’ve increased the knowledge available to humanity. Just like that. Let’s spread that idea.

Measurement is controversial. It has pros and cons. But when you’re measuring the right things, it can be incredibly powerful. This OA Week, join us in measuring the right things. Find your [\#OAscore](https://twitter.com/search?f=tweets&q=%23OAscore), make it better, tweet it out. If we’re going to [measure steps](https://blog.fitbit.com/the-magic-of-10000-steps/), let’s make them steps that matter.
