## Quick bio

Brent Hecht is **Partner Director of Applied Science at Microsoft** (Experiences & Devices), where
he leads a team of researchers working on AI in productivity scenarios and co-leads Microsoft's
cross-company "New Future of Work" initiative. He holds an additional appointment as an **Affiliate
Associate Professor at Northwestern University**, where he was an Associate Professor before joining
Microsoft in 2019 and where he leads the **People, Space, and Algorithms (PSA) Research Group**.
Sources: [Microsoft Research profile](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/people/brhecht/),
[brenthecht.com](https://brenthecht.com/), and his own CV
([bhecht_cv.pdf](https://brenthecht.com/bhecht_cv.pdf), saved in full at
`by/2025--brent-hecht-curriculum-vitae.md`; its most recent entry is ACM Distinguished Member, 2025).

He was previously Assistant Professor at the University of Minnesota (2013–2016) and holds a PhD in
Computer Science from Northwestern (2013, Best Dissertation Award), an MA in Geography from UC Santa
Barbara, and a BA in Computer Science and Geography from Macalester College. His research sits at
the intersection of human–computer interaction, social computing, and spatial computing. Recurring
threads in the corpus below: geographic and linguistic bias in user-generated content
(Wikipedia, OpenStreetMap, geotagged social media); the dependence of search engines and AI systems
on Wikipedia and other user-generated content; **"data leverage," "data strikes," "data dividends,"
and "data labor"** — the bargaining power data contributors hold over technology companies, much of
it co-authored with **Nicholas Vincent** (also on the summit roster); the ACM/NeurIPS
broader-impact-statement push (he co-led the "It's Time to Do Something" peer-review proposal); and,
at Microsoft, remote/hybrid work and the productivity effects of LLM-based tools. He was on the
founding executive committee of ACM FAccT.
