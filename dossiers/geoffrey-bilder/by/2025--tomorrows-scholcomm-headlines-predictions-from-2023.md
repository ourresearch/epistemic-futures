I recently ran across a screenshot from November 2023.

### Background[#](#background)

One of our product managers was a representative on the [STM Future Lab](https://stm-assoc.org/what-we-do/strategic-areas/standards-technology/future-lab/) group. They had been asked to predict the ScholComm headlines in a few years time— and so they checked if their colleagues had any ideas. There were a few safe (albeit depressing) predictions based on clear trends (e.g. an AI-generated paper gets accepted to a prestigious journal, more “paper mills" appear, more bulk retractions are issued).

The above was my take. As I recall, most people thought it was pretty unrealistic. I don’t think it was ever presented to STM.

At least by posting it here, it gets a DOI via [Rogue Scholar](https://rogue-scholar.org)— and we can wait to see if/when it gets blocked.

I’ve been concerned about the concentration of critical open scholarly infrastructure in the US for over a decade. I have ideas. And more. If you’re also concerned *and* are in a position to help people do something about it, [get in touch](https://gbilder.com/about/).
