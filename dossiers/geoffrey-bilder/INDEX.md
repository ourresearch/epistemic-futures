## Quick bio

Geoffrey Bilder is a scholarly-technology architect best known for eighteen years at **Crossref**, which he joined in January 2007 as its first Director of Strategic Initiatives (later Director of Technology & Research) and where he led the technical development and launch of Similarity Check/CrossCheck, Crossmark, ORCID and the Open Funder Registry; he was seconded from Crossref to be ORCID's first director of technology in 2010. Earlier he co-founded Brown University's Scholarly Technology Group (1993), ran IT R&D at Monitor Group, was CTO of Ingenta (2002–2005), and consulted at Scholarly Information Strategies. With Jennifer Lin and Cameron Neylon he wrote the **Principles of Open Scholarly Infrastructure (POSI)** in 2015 — now adopted by ~30 infrastructure organisations — and he has continued to write about POSI compliance, "fauxpen" infrastructure, persistent identifiers, trust signals and AI from his personal blog *Louche Cannon* ([gbilder.com](https://gbilder.com/)). He lives in Nîmes, France.

### Conventions notes for this dossier

- **Stub/pointer posts.** Six short `gbilder.com` posts are pure pointers to a paper, PDF or video elsewhere (POSI, Identify This!, the ORCID white paper, the Fenner interview, the ORCID identifier structure, the Fauxpen talk). They are saved as `full-text` blog-posts (the pointer text is genuinely all there is, and it usually carries a paragraph of framing context he wrote later), and the thing pointed at is saved as its own item where fetchable. Each such file says so in `notes:`.
- **Slide decks.** Presentation PDFs (bollockschain, DOI Myths, OSI slides, LIVE21) are `type: report`, `content: full-text` where the extraction recovers the deck's whole text, with a `notes:` caveat that images/diagrams are not represented. The Seoul 2017 deck is image-only; **`pdftotext` recovered 253 characters, OCR (PDFKit + Vision) recovered 5,547** and the file now carries the OCR text, still labelled `content: excerpt` because 8 of its 20 slides yielded no recognisable text. That file also carries an explicit caution: two of its slides are screenshots of a **third-party journal article** (Gjerde & Onishi, *Journal of Research on Adolescence*) shown as an illustration, and the OCR of those pages is **not Bilder's words**. It was the dossier's only genuine OCR candidate — every other PDF-sourced item extracts well above the ~500-byte floor (checked 2026-08-13).
- **Unbylined Crossref posts.** `by/2008--crossref-claddier-final-report.md` is bylined to Crossref's generic `admin` account. It is attributed to Bilder on **internal evidence** — first-person reference to a talk he gave, linking a slide deck filed as `PresentationBilder.pdf` — and the hedge is carried in the `authors:` field itself ("unbylined; attributed on internal evidence"), not only in `notes:`, per the fan-out ruling that downstream reads `authors:` as fact.
- **"What Color is Your Paratext?"** is a conference report of his NASIG 2009 vision session written up by a *recorder* (Andrée J. Rathemacher). Filed in `by/` because the payload is his talk, with the recorder's role stated in `authors:` and `notes:`.
- **Duplicate-content pairs.** Where the same text exists as both preprint and version of record (the preprints paper; the software/data citation paper), the version of record is `full-text` and the preprint is `abstract-only` with a cross-reference, so the same prose is not stored twice. The one deliberate exception is POSI v1, kept both as the site-archived text and as the citable figshare PDF deposit, because those are the two artefacts the community actually cites.
